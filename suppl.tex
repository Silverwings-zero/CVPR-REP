\section{Data and protocol}
\paragraph{Recording.} A GelSight Mini (native 1640$\times$1232, \SI{11.3}{\micro\metre}/px, \SI{18.8}{fps}) sits on one finger of a two-finger parallel gripper carried by a 7-DoF Franka Emika Panda arm, and an ATI Nano17 on the other finger (host-timestamped at 130--190\,Hz). Target normal forces are 0.5--5\,N and speed commands 10--100\,mm/s (see Motion below). Campaigns: A, June 15--22 (15 sessions); B, July 6--8 (15); C, July 9--13 (5); D, September 22 to October 15 (20). Every session holds one material. Three sessions hold a different specimen than the other sessions of their material: textured rubber and EPDM foam in campaign D, and r\'esum\'e paper in campaign A. Wood was grasped at another height in campaign D. All four stay in the 55-session results. \EpRecorded\ episodes were recorded and accepted. \EpNoCrops\ episodes of campaign D have no span of eight consecutive frames in contact inside the stroke window, so the material experiments use \EpCrops\ episodes (\EpTrainABC\ in A--C, \EpTestD\ in D). Hardware: unit 1 with gel 1 from June 15 to July 9 (S1), unit 2 with gel 2 from July 10 to October 9, 18:42 (S2), unit 2 with gel 3 from the cork session of October 9, 19:37 (S3). Campaign D also recorded bare-gel frames (a start frame and lubricant pre/post pairs) and, in seven sessions, a 3-s bare-gel lead-in before every grasp.
\paragraph{Motion.} The gripper's two fingers are driven by separate position-controlled servos. For each episode they close in small steps until the Nano17 reads the target force, and their width is then held, so the force during a stroke is set by the grasp and is not regulated. The arm runs a Cartesian impedance controller, and the collector moves its target in steps of 1\,mm, or up to 2\,mm for speed commands above 50\,mm/s, with a dwell per step that sets the speed. An episode strokes 2\,cm in $+x$, $-x$, $+z$ and $-z$ along the face, returning to the center after each stroke, and then follows a circle of 2\,cm radius in 36 steps at a speed command of at most 40\,mm/s. A stroke is aborted when the force reaches 7\,N. The two target forces of a material are 40 and 80\pct\ of the largest normal force in its model in the Penn Haptic Texture Toolkit (HaTT)~\cite{culbertson2014hatt}, limited to 5\,N (\cref{tab:forces}). Two materials are exceptions. For vinyl the largest force is that of its HaTT recording, because we had no fitted model for it. Fiberglass is not among the toolkit's 100 textures. Its model was recorded later in the toolkit's format. The speed commands are 50, 70 and 100\,mm/s in campaign A (38 episodes per session: two forces, three speeds, five repeats, and four episodes per force whose strokes each draw a command between 10 and 100\,mm/s), 10, 25 and 35\,mm/s in campaign B (30 episodes), and all six in campaigns C and D (68 episodes). The realized speed is lower than the command. Over the episodes of the six commands, the 90th percentile of the speed during motion has a median of \SpdPeakTen, \SpdPeakTwentyFive, \SpdPeakThirtyFive, \SpdPeakFifty, \SpdPeakSeventy\ and \SpdPeakHundred\,mm/s. Every five episodes the gripper opens, the arm lifts off the box and an operator re-lubricates the gel with petroleum jelly (Vaseline). Campaign D records a bare-gel frame before and after each of these pauses.

\paragraph{Data and code.} We will release all \EpRecorded\ episodes with native video, force, kinematics, bare-gel frames and metadata, plus the splits, the pre-registration file and the code (CC BY 4.0 for the data, MIT for the code).
\paragraph{Crops.} Every episode yields twelve crop tubes of 192\,px at native resolution. Four start frames are spread evenly over the frames in contact inside the stroke window, and three positions are drawn at random for each, at least 16\pct\ of the frame's height and width away from its border. A tube holds eight consecutive frames at one position, and markers are inpainted in every frame. The models of the paper's tables read single frames of these tubes. In training, a sample is a random frame of a random tube. At test time an episode is read through the first and the fifth frame of each of its twelve tubes, 24 crops in all, and their log-probabilities are averaged. The models of the earlier protocol (\cref{sec:earlier}) read the first frame of each tube, twelve crops per episode, and their eight-frame variant sums a tube's eight frames exactly. Force and contact models read whole native frames.
\paragraph{State estimate.} In the paper's tables, a session's mean contact frame $M_s$ is the mean of its whole contact frames, four per episode, taken at the 10th, 40th, 70th and 95th percentiles of the normal force among the episode's frames in contact, with the markers as recorded. $G$ is the mean of the training sessions' $M_s$, and a crop is corrected with the part of $L_\sigma(\hat F - G)$ at its position in the frame. The earlier protocol builds a session's state field from its crops instead: the position-wise sum and count of the session's contact crops in frame coordinates, shrunk toward the all-session mean with weight 2 where few crops cover a pixel. A crop's own episode is left out when its own session is the source, and $G$ is the mean over the training sessions.
\paragraph{Training in the paper's tables.} The ResNet-50 starts from torchvision's ImageNet weights, and a linear classifier replaces its last layer. A crop is converted from BGR to RGB and normalized with the ImageNet statistics. Training minimizes the cross-entropy with label smoothing 0.1 for 6{,}000 steps of AdamW at batch size 64. The learning rate of $3\cdot10^{-4}$ warms up linearly over the first 300 steps and follows a cosine decay to zero, with weight decay 0.05, gradient norms clipped at 1, and mixed precision. Each training sample draws a class, then one of the class's training sessions, then one of that session's episodes, so that every class and every session of a class appear equally often. The augmentations are flips, quarter turns and photometric jitter, a gain drawn for each color channel between 0.8 and 1.2. A method that uses the stream draws a second batch of 64 crops from it at every step, uniformly over the stream's episodes and with the same augmentations. A discriminator, where a method has one, is trained by AdamW at the same learning rate without a schedule. Every row of the paper's tables averages seeds 0 to 2, and the differences marked with five seeds use seeds 0 to 4.

The other encoders of \cref{tab:encoders} use the same settings on tube crops. ConvNeXt-T starts from torchvision's ImageNet weights. DINOv2-B is fine-tuned as well. It reads the top-left 182\,px of a crop, the largest multiple of its 14-px patch, and its feature is the average of the class token and the mean patch token. The CNN is the encoder described below, trained from scratch. In the frame view (\cref{tab:frameview}), a training crop of 384\,px, or 392\,px for DINOv2-B, is drawn at a random position at least 64\,px from the border of one of the episode's four whole contact frames, with the markers as recorded. An episode is tested on 48 crops, a grid of three by four positions in each of the four frames. The learning rate is $10^{-4}$ for ResNet-50 and ConvNeXt-T, and $2\cdot10^{-5}$ at batch size 32 for DINOv2-B.
\paragraph{The CNN, frozen encoders and heads.} The CNN's input is the BGR crop, two band-pass detail images (the crop minus its 9-px and 33-px box means) and two log color ratios high-passed at 33\,px (11 channels). Four stages of two $3\times3$ convolutions with BatchNorm and GELU and a blur-pool downsampling (widths 32, 64, 96, 128) are followed by global average pooling and a linear map to 192 dimensions. In the earlier protocol it is trained with the cross-entropy for 12 epochs of AdamW (learning rate $3\cdot10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-3}$, cosine schedule) at batch size 48. Sparsh (ViT-B/16) runs on native 192-px crops ($12\times12$ tokens) with its sinusoidal position embeddings regenerated for that grid and its own two-frame input (frames 7 and 2 of an eight-frame crop). Every encoder reads the same native crops, so that all rows of \cref{tab:earlier,tab:earlierenc} see the same pixels. Sparsh was pretrained on whole frames at a lower resolution with a background frame subtracted, and we did not evaluate it in that protocol. DINOv2-base runs on the central 182\,px of the eight-frame mean with ImageNet normalization. Features are mean-pooled final tokens (768-d). The earlier protocol classifies with MLP heads (256 hidden units, dropout 0.2, 40 epochs of AdamW) fitted on the standardized embeddings of a frozen encoder, with three seeds and the logits summed per episode.

\paragraph{Seeds.} Over seeds, the 55-session mean varies by under a point for source-only training and the correction and by one to three points for the aligned models, less than the intervals over sessions.

\section{The tracker}
\label{sec:tracker}
\Cref{alg:tracker} states the image correction of the paper's method section in full. In the paper's tables the dates of the two hardware changes are given, so the test for a change uses these dates. The detector takes their place only in the experiment of \cref{sec:detector}.
\begin{algorithm}[h]
\caption{Correcting the robot's stream for its hardware state}
\label{alg:tracker}
\small\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require training mean $G$; Gaussian low-pass $L_\sigma$; change detector with thresholds from the training data
\State $\mathcal S \gets \emptyset$ \Comment{mean contact frames of the sessions since the last change}
\For{each session of the stream (one object, no label)}
  \State $\hat F \gets \big(G + \sum_{M\in\mathcal S} M\big) / \big(1 + |\mathcal S|\big)$
  \For{each episode of the session}
    \State \textbf{correct} every contact crop: $x'(p) \gets x(p) - L_\sigma\big(\hat F - G\big)(p)$ \Comment{to the encoder}
    \If{the detector declares a change of hardware}
      \State $\mathcal S \gets \emptyset$; \ $\hat F \gets G$
    \EndIf
  \EndFor
  \State add the session's mean contact frame to $\mathcal S$
\EndFor
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}
\paragraph{Settings.} Recent window $K=8$ episodes, reference window $W=30$. Fine statistic: correlation of the 190--370\,\si{\micro\metre} bands (difference of 17-px and 33-px box means of the gray image) of the two window means, over pixels at least 60\,px from every marker of the sessions in the windows. Illumination statistic: mean absolute difference of the two window means after a Gaussian blur with $\sigma=33$\,px. Rule, fixed before evaluation: $\tau_f$ is the 10th percentile and $\tau_i$ the 95th percentile of the statistics over the first hardware state's episodes (on whole frames $\tau_f=\FrTauF$, $\tau_i=\FrTauI$ gray levels). An alarm needs $m=5$ consecutive episodes beyond both; detection then pauses for $W+K$ episodes. The estimate is that of \cref{alg:tracker}: the mean of the sessions since the last change, with $G$ counted as one more session, and after a change it restarts from $G$ alone. The earlier protocol (\cref{sec:earlier}) restarted differently. After an alarm its prior became $G$ plus the Gaussian-blurred ($\sigma=33$\,px) difference between the old estimate and $G$, which carries the old illumination over to the new hardware. \Cref{sec:variants} tests the restart from $G$ alone in that protocol, under the name prior $G$. Markers are detected on the first frame of each session's first raw video (\texttt{scripts/build\_session\_dots.py}).
\paragraph{Detector input.} The detector reads the mean of an episode's four whole contact frames (native, markers excluded as above). Our first implementation computed the same statistics on the twelve crops per episode of the earlier protocol, which cover each frame sparsely, so that an eight-episode window holds one object seen through a few crops per pixel. With the same rule ($\tau_f=\TrkTauF$, $\tau_i=\TrkTauI$) it raised \TrkAlarms\ alarm on the four-month stream, \TrkGelLatency\ episodes after the gel replacement, and missed the change of unit: there the illumination statistic stayed beyond its threshold for \TrkUnitIllumRun\ episodes but the fine statistic for at most \TrkUnitFineRun. On spliced streams it detected \TeOursDet\pct\ of the changes. Correcting campaign D with its restarts gave \SrcSupTrkAcc\pct\ for the CNN when run since June, because the estimate for the September sessions still averaged objects of the first unit, and \SrcSupTrkDepAcc\pct\ when started in September. A per-object variant on the crops, which compares each new object's mean field after four episodes with the mean of the other objects since the last change, tested \ObjTested\ objects, raised \ObjAlarms\ alarm, a false one, and caught neither change. Whole frames, the input the detector uses on the public datasets, fixed the problem without changing the rule. This was verified on the stream that had revealed the problem and on spliced streams from the same recordings. Before the percentile rule, an earlier version of the detector chose its thresholds from a scan of 210 settings on our stream. The windows ($K$, $W$), the persistence $m$, the band and the blur come from that version.
\paragraph{Spliced streams.} For each public dataset the same statistics are computed with the band and blur converted to its pixel size, on whole images with dark marker blobs masked. Streams consist of $W+K+10$ items of one hardware state followed by $2W$ items of another state (100 streams) or of other recordings of the same state (100 streams). Thresholds use the same percentiles, computed on one unit of each dataset: our S1, SITR Mini 0, FEATS the training split's main unit, and Tactile MNIST gel 1 in its first three runs. A change counts as detected if an alarm falls within $3W$ items. Items keep their recording order on our data (one object per session) and on Tactile MNIST (runs in order). SITR and FEATS images are in random order, which mixes objects within a window and makes their test easier. Tactile MNIST has one gel swap, tested in both directions. FEATS has one unit with an old and a new gel and a second unit with two gels, and SITR has four Minis. The calibration unit also appears in the test streams. \Cref{tab:roc} shows the trade-off for other percentiles. On the real four-month stream with whole frames, the 1st\,/\,99th percentiles detect the unit and the gel change after \FrSweepOneUnit\ and \FrSweepOneGel\ episodes with \FrSweepOneFalse\ false alarms, the 5th\,/\,95th with \FrSweepFiveFalse, the 10th\,/\,90th with \FrSweepTenFalse\ and the 20th\,/\,80th with \FrSweepTwentyFalse.

\section{Hand-picked state sources}
The previous session's state is the mean contact field of the session recorded just before the test session, whichever object it held. The bare-gel correction uses the marker-inpainted bare-gel frames that campaign D recorded before each session: $\hat F_s = \bar F + (B_s - \bar B)$, where $B_s$ is the session's mean bare-gel frame, $\bar B$ the mean of these over campaign D, and $\bar F$ the mean contact field of campaign D's sessions. A bare-gel frame alone differs from a contact frame by the gel's deformation and lighting under load, so it is placed relative to the contact mean. That mean uses the whole test campaign, which makes the correction non-causal. Campaigns A--C hold no bare-gel frame: their recordings start after the grasp has closed, with \AbcFirstContactPct\pct\ of \AbcEpisodes\ episodes above 0.3\,N in their first frame.

\section{Adaptation baselines}
\paragraph{Alignment in the main paper.} CDAN+E~\cite{long2018cdan} follows the Transfer-Learning-Library implementation. A domain discriminator receives the outer product of the encoder's features and the classifier's detached predictions. When this product has more than 4096 dimensions, it is randomly projected to 1024. Each sample is weighted by $1 + e^{-H(\hat y)}$, so that confident predictions count more, and the encoder is trained against the discriminator through gradient reversal with a warm-start schedule. The objective is the cross-entropy on the labeled training crops plus the discriminator's weighted loss, with weight one. In the fold holding out campaign D, the alignment stream contains configurations S2 and S3, which are treated as a single target domain.
\paragraph{Confidence objectives.} Two objectives ask the classifier to be confident on the new hardware while spreading its predictions over the classes. Applied to the average prediction of several contacts, as MEMO~\cite{zhang2022memo} does for augmented copies, the objective adds a few points with an interval that includes zero (row f of the paper's ablation). Applied to every contact, as SHOT~\cite{liang2020shot} does, it lowers accuracy by about five points (row g). The method uses neither.
\paragraph{Test-time baselines.} The five test-time methods of the paper adapt the source-only model on the stream in recording order, without source data. A step takes three consecutive episodes, 72 crops, which is the methods' batch of 64 rounded to whole episodes, and every crop is predicted before the update it takes part in. The model returns to the source weights at each change of hardware. Per-batch BatchNorm~\cite{schneider2020bn} normalizes with the statistics of the current batch. Tent~\cite{wang2021tent} minimizes the prediction entropy over the affine parameters of the normalization layers by SGD (learning rate $2.5\cdot10^{-4}$, momentum 0.9). SAR~\cite{niu2023sar} adds a sharpness-aware step ($\rho=0.05$), updates only on predictions with an entropy below $0.4\ln C$ for $C$ classes, leaves the deepest block unchanged, and returns to the source weights when its moving entropy falls below 0.2. DeYO~\cite{lee2024deyo} updates on crops with an entropy below $0.5\ln C$ whose confidence falls by more than 0.2 when their $4\times4$ patches are shuffled. RoTTA~\cite{yuan2023rotta} is described in \cref{sec:rotta}. Here it updates every 64 crops, and flips and quarter turns replace its affine warp, so that no crop is resampled. These are the papers' settings for a ResNet-50 at batch size 64.
\paragraph{Test-time baselines on shuffled streams.} With the episodes of each configuration shuffled, as in their own benchmarks, per-batch BatchNorm, Tent, SAR and DeYO recover to \XpTtaBnIidAcc\pct, just above the unadapted model.
\paragraph{Published retraining methods.} MCC, SDAT and MIC keep the settings of their papers and share the encoder, optimizer, schedule and budget of the paper's tables. MCC uses a temperature of 2.5 and a weight of one. SDAT is run in its strongest classification setting, CDAN with minimum class confusion and a sharpness-aware step ($\rho=0.02$) on the classification loss. MIC adds to SDAT its masked consistency loss with weight one, with 64-px blocks masked at a ratio of 0.7 and a teacher averaged with momentum 0.9. DANN does not follow its paper's schedule. Its discriminator is a two-layer network with 256 hidden units on the pooled features, and the weight of its gradient reversal rises linearly to 0.3 over the first 1{,}800 steps.
\paragraph{SDAT and MIC.} Both papers give the adversarial and the class-confusion losses a weight of one. Each method was also run with a weight of \XpSdatWLowValue\ on both losses, and the weight that scored higher on the first seed with the correction is reported for all seeds. With a weight of one, SDAT scores \XpSdatWOneSeedZero\pct\ and MIC \XpMicWOneSeedZero\pct\ on that seed. With \XpSdatWLowValue\ they score \XpSdatWLowSeedZero\ and \XpMicWLowSeedZero\pct, so the tables report both methods with a weight of \XpSdatMicWeight. Our method keeps the weight of one for CDAN+E in every run.
\paragraph{FDA.} The published method closest to the correction is FDA~\cite{fda2020}, which gives every training image the low-frequency amplitude spectrum of an image from the stream, so that the model trains under the new hardware's illumination. With $\beta=0.01$, which swaps the band that the correction removes, FDA gains about six points over source-only training, half of what the correction gains without retraining (\XdReadVsFda\ points between them, \XdReadVsFdaLo\ to \XdReadVsFdaHi). The correction adds about three points to FDA (\XpFdaAcc\pct\ [\XpFdaLo, \XpFdaHi] alone, \XpFdaReadAcc\pct\ [\XpFdaReadLo, \XpFdaReadHi] with the correction), and the wider band of $\beta=0.05$ gains less.
\paragraph{Baselines of the earlier protocol.} All baselines use the trained single-frame CNNs with heads fitted on raw training embeddings (three head seeds, three encoder seeds). Label-shift correction: EM re-estimation of the class priors from the episode posteriors of the whole test campaign~\cite{saerens2002labelshift}, applied as a logit offset. AdaBN~\cite{li2018adabn}: BatchNorm statistics re-estimated on the test crops of each hardware state, using the true change date. The causal version uses only the crops of the state seen so far. Tent~\cite{wang2021tent}: entropy minimization of the BatchNorm affine parameters with batch statistics (Adam, lr $10^{-3}$), reset at the change date. Online, it takes one step per episode in recording order on that episode's twelve crops. With mixed batches, the crops of a hardware state are shuffled into batches of 64 for one pass and then predicted with the adapted parameters, which a robot cannot do. LAME~\cite{boudiaf2022lame} adjusts the head's predictions over a graph that links each crop to its five nearest neighbors in feature space, on batches of 64 consecutive crops in recording order. \Cref{tab:earlier} gives the results.


\section{Corrections without a state estimate}
Three CNNs differ from the plain CNN only in their input or training, with three seeds each and the same 12 epochs. Instance normalization standardizes each input crop per channel over its pixels and maps it to $0.5+0.15z$. The high-pass input is $x-\mathrm{blur}_{\sigma=33\,\mathrm{px}}(x)+0.5$ per channel, which removes the smooth illumination field. A Fourier amplitude swap after FDA~\cite{fda2020} replaces, with probability 0.5 during training, the low-frequency amplitude spectrum ($\beta=0.01$) of a training crop with that of another crop in the batch. FDA itself takes the spectrum from an unlabeled target image. Corrected with \cref{alg:tracker}, their training images are corrected relative to the same-state reference like every other model. A fourth CNN is trained with state augmentation: with probability 0.5 a training crop is re-rendered under the state field of another training session, $x - F_s + F_{s'}$. \Cref{tab:earlier} gives the results.

\section{Variants of the estimate}
\label{sec:variants}
All variants share the detector and its alarms. Uniform weighting averages contacts rather than objects. The decay variant multiplies the weight of every earlier object by $\lambda=0.5^{1/2}$ each time a new object begins, including the one-object prior, so that the estimate forgets an object after a few others. Prior $G$ restarts the estimate after an alarm from the training mean alone, without the old smooth illumination. The September variants start the stream at the first session of campaign D, after the change of unit, with and without restarts at later alarms. On campaign D (plain CNN\,/\,jitter CNN): object weighting \af{\FoDSupTrkStateAcc}{\FoDAugTrkStateAcc}, uniform \af{\FoDSupUniStateAcc}{\FoDAugUniStateAcc}, decay \af{\FoDSupDecayStateAcc}{\FoDAugDecayStateAcc}, prior $G$ \af{\FoDSupPriorGStateAcc}{\FoDAugPriorGStateAcc}. The paired bootstrap over test materials puts the difference between object weighting and decay between $\FoDSupCiVsDecayLo$ and $+$\FoDSupCiVsDecayHi\ points for the plain CNN. Two variants were added after the method was fixed. The decay variant without any detector (no restarts) gives \af{\FoDSupDecayNoDetStateAcc}{\FoDAugDecayNoDetStateAcc} on campaign D and \af{\FoABRevSupDecayNoDetStateAcc}{\FoABRevAugDecayNoDetStateAcc} on the reversed split, where its quick forgetting of the training prior, which comes from other hardware, helps. Leaving the current object out of its own estimate gives \af{\FoDSupOtherStateAcc}{\FoDAugOtherStateAcc} on campaign D, with the four rigid materials at \af{\FoDSupOtherStateTwo}{\FoDAugOtherStateTwo} instead of \af{\FoDSupTrkStateTwo}{\FoDAugTrkStateTwo}, and \af{\FoABRevSupOtherStateAcc}{\FoABRevAugOtherStateAcc} on the reversed split. Correcting only the test images, with heads fitted on raw training images, gives \af{\FoDSupRawHeadAcc}{\FoDAugRawHeadAcc} on campaign D and \af{\FoABRevSupRawHeadAcc}{\FoABRevAugRawHeadAcc} on the reversed split. The mean of all test images of each true hardware state (non-causal) gives \af{\FoDSupStateMeanStateAcc}{\FoDAugStateMeanStateAcc}.

\section{The pre-registered reversed test}
The protocol was written and committed (\texttt{docs/PREREG\_REVERSE\_TEST.md}, commit \texttt{659e98a}) before any model of the reversed split was trained, with every setting frozen as of the paper's commit \texttt{60e826b}. \section{Sparsh in its own protocol}
\label{sec:sparshown}
Sparsh's GelSight pretraining reads two whole frames five apart, resizes them to $224\times224$, and subtracts a no-contact background as $\mathrm{clip}((I-B)/255+0.5,\,0,\,1)$ before concatenating them to six channels. We follow this exactly (\texttt{scripts/sparsh\_native.py}). For every episode the four whole contact frames used elsewhere serve as $I_t$, and the frame five earlier is decoded from the raw video. Logits of the four frames are summed. Campaigns A--C hold no bare-gel frame, so training frames use the mean of the other training sessions of their hardware epoch as the background. Test frames use either the session's bare-gel frame, Sparsh's own practice, or \cref{alg:tracker}'s estimate computed on the same downscaled whole frames. Accuracy\,/\,top-class share, raw\,/\,bare gel\,/\,\cref{alg:tracker}: Sparsh-I-JEPA \af{\SnIjepaRawAcc}{\SnIjepaRawTop}, \af{\SnIjepaBlankAcc}{\SnIjepaBlankTop}, \af{\SnIjepaStateAcc}{\SnIjepaStateTop}; Sparsh-DINO \af{\SnSdinoRawAcc}{\SnSdinoRawTop}, \af{\SnSdinoBlankAcc}{\SnSdinoBlankTop}, \af{\SnSdinoStateAcc}{\SnSdinoStateTop}; Sparsh-MAE \af{\SnMaeRawAcc}{\SnMaeRawTop}, \af{\SnMaeBlankAcc}{\SnMaeBlankTop}, \af{\SnMaeStateAcc}{\SnMaeStateTop}. The raw rows give Sparsh an input it was not pretrained on. \section{RoTTA and the ResNet-18 baseline}
\label{sec:rotta}
RoTTA~\cite{yuan2023rotta} replaces every BatchNorm layer with a robust one that normalizes with global statistics, moved with momentum 0.05 by the memory bank's statistics. It keeps a category-balanced memory of 64 test crops, ranked by age and prediction entropy, and predicts with a teacher that is a moving average (0.001) of a student. After every 64 test crops the student's affine BatchNorm parameters take an Adam step (learning rate $10^{-3}$) on a timeliness-weighted cross-entropy between the teacher's prediction and the student's prediction on a strongly augmented copy (photometric jitter and flips). As for Tent, the stream is reset at the true gel replacement, and three encoder seeds and three head seeds are averaged (\texttt{scripts/tta\_rotta.py}). The ResNet-18 starts from torchvision's ImageNet weights, reads the BGR crop as RGB with ImageNet normalization, and is fine-tuned with the CNN's settings (12 epochs, AdamW, three seeds). Its row in \cref{tab:earlierenc} uses the protocol of \cref{tab:earlier}. With the previous session's state as the training reference instead, \cref{alg:tracker} gives \FoDResTrkPrevAcc\pct.

\section{Label-free adaptation of a frozen encoder}
Each training crop is re-rendered under another session's state, $x' = x - F_s(p) + F_{s'}(p)$. A small MLP on the frozen Sparsh features (768--768--256) is trained with InfoNCE to give $x$ and $x'$ similar embeddings, and to separate $x$ from a decoy: another crop's residual $x_d - F_{s_d}(p)$ placed under $x$'s own state and position, $F_s(p)$. Batches are drawn from a few sessions so that the other samples in a batch share the anchor's state. The adapter uses no material labels, but it uses each crop's session, which in our data identifies the object. \section{HCT}
We use the HCT frames of TVL~\cite{tvl2024} with the material labels and the released few-shot split of \cite{mohsan2026language}: \HctFrames\ frames from \HctSessions\ sessions on \HctDays\ days, \HctClasses\ material classes, native $480\times640$ frames cropped to $476\times630$ for DINOv2 ViT-B/14. The session folders of the release are named by their Unix time and run from December 13, 2023 to January 15, 2024, which is the recording span of about one month quoted in the related work of the paper. \HctTestSessInTrain\ of the \HctTestSessions\ test sessions of the released split also appear in its training set. Session protocol: five folds over sessions. Day protocol: each day held out in turn, classes absent from the training days dropped, days with fewer than 20 test frames or one class skipped. Inputs: raw, background ($x - B_s + \bar B$ with the session's released no-contact frame $B_s$), and stream, the per-object running mean of \cref{alg:tracker} within the test day (sessions in time order, each session one object, the training mean as a one-object prior), with training frames corrected relative to the mean of the other training sessions of their day. Heads: MLPs with three seeds, macro accuracy over classes. Raw\,/\,background accuracy is \HctPubRawAcc\,/\,\HctPubBgAcc\ on the published split and \HctSessRawAcc\,/\,\HctSessBgAcc\ with sessions held out. With days held out, raw\,/\,background\,/\,stream give \HctDayRawAcc\,/\,\HctDayBgAcc\,/\,\HctDayStreamAcc\ (top-class share \HctDayRawTop, \HctDayBgTop\ and \HctDayStreamTop\pct). The no-contact frame alone, through a logistic regression on DINOv2 features left out one session at a time, gives the material at \HctProbeLoso\pct\ when sessions of the same day are in training and \HctProbeOtherDays\pct\ when they are not (most frequent class \HctProbeMajority\pct).

\section{Force and contact}
\paragraph{Normal force in brief.}
Force is a matter of mechanics, and the state does not describe it. Whole-frame models trained on June and July to regress normal force from appearance reach $R^2\le\ForceLearnedBestR$ on September and October, even with the state removed. Marker displacements against a bare-gel grid recorded in September, fitted on June and July, predict the September and October sessions before the gel replacement at $r=\ForceGeomXeraC$ ($R^2=\ForceGeomXeraR$).

\begin{figure}[h]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig/fig_force.pdf}
\caption{$R^2$ of log normal force on September--October for models trained on June--July.}
\end{figure}
Marker grids are detected on the bare gel at native resolution (63--64 dots, the Mini's $7\times9$ grid), matched to each contact frame's dots within 0.6 grid spacings, and summarized by the affine fit of the displacement field (divergence, mean displacement, net shear). Within a session the divergence follows the Nano17 force at Spearman \ForceWithinRho. Fitted on the first 13 sessions of campaign D, the linear map predicts the last 7 at $R^2$ \ForceGeomFreshR\ ($r$ \ForceGeomFreshC) with a fresh bare-gel grid and \ForceGeomPrevR\ ($r$ \ForceGeomPrevC) with the previous session's grid. A new gel keeps the force ranking but changes the scale from displacement to force, so absolute force needs one scale calibration per gel. Contact: detectors are trained on the seven lead-in sessions (lead-in and pre-contact frames are no contact, onset and contact frames are contact) and tested on the other sessions' contact frames and bare-gel frames. Referencing the embedding to the session's own bare gel gives \ContactRefSpec\pct\ specificity at \ContactRefRecall\pct\ recall. Thresholded raw differencing against the bare gel gives \ContactBlankdiffSpecMin--\ContactBlankdiffSpecMax\pct\ specificity, depending on the lead-in frames available to fit its threshold.

\section{Measurement details}
The camera's exposure and white balance are fixed: on a held contact the mean of each color channel drifts by at most 0.3 gray levels per episode. Warping a bare-gel frame by the membrane's measured displacement lowers its match to the session's mean contact frame from \BlankVsFieldFar\ to \WarpedBlankVsFieldFar, which suggests that the fine pattern is fixed in the camera's view and does not move with the membrane. The membrane moves by a median of only \WarpDispPx\,px, however, so this test is weak. The signature uses pixels covered by at least three crops of the partner field and at least 60\,px from every marker of the session. With the marker surroundings included, the same-hardware medians are about 0.05 higher and the cross-hardware floor rises from \HwFarOtherUnit\ to \HwOtherUnit, which is inpainting residue. Partner categories follow the hardware timeline: own session (episode left out), another object the same day, the same unit and gel on another day within a month, the same unit and gel three months apart (July 10--13 against September 28 to October 9), the other unit, and the other gel of the same unit. The session of September 22 is not used as a three-month partner, for the same reason as in the probe, and its own value, 0.11, is part of the median. Real Tactile MNIST: bands of 3--7\,px, its own run (object left out), other runs of the same day, other days of the same gel, and the other gel.
Within a session, the illumination field changes by a median of \DriftUse\ gray levels over \DriftUseContacts\ grasps. Between the last bare-gel frame of one session and the first of the next, it changes by \DriftIdle\ gray levels despite no intervening contact, so the field can vary while the sensor is idle, rather than only through use.

\section{Results of the earlier protocol}
\label{sec:earlier}
Several sections above report an earlier protocol, which preceded the 55-session protocol of the paper. It trains on campaigns A to C and tests on campaign D, like the paper's fold that holds out D. A CNN is trained on single frames, its encoder is then frozen, and MLP heads are fitted on its embeddings. The correction subtracts the whole estimate, without the low-pass filter. It also corrects the training crops, each relative to the mean of the other training sessions recorded between the same two detector alarms, except in the row marked test images only. The estimate runs over the four-month stream and restarts at the detector's alarms (\cref{sec:tracker}).
