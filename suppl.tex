\section{Data and protocol}
\paragraph{Recording.} A GelSight Mini (native 1640$\times$1232, \SI{11.3}{\micro\metre}/px, \SI{18.8}{fps}) sits on one finger of a Franka parallel gripper and an ATI Nano17 on the other (host-timestamped at 130--190\,Hz). Commanded normal force is 0.9--5\,N and the stroke speed 10--100\,mm/s. Campaigns: A, June 15--22 (15 sessions); B, July 6--8 (15); C, July 9--13 (5); D, September 22 to October 15 (20). Every session holds one material. \EpRecorded\ episodes were recorded and accepted. \EpNoCrops\ episodes of campaign D have no span of eight consecutive frames in contact inside the stroke window, so the material experiments use \EpCrops\ episodes (\EpTrainABC\ in A--C, \EpTestD\ in D). Hardware: unit 1 with gel 1 from June 15 to July 9 (S1), unit 2 with gel 2 from July 10 to October 9, 18:42 (S2), unit 2 with gel 3 from the cork session of October 9, 19:37 (S3). The laboratory log recorded four gel replacements and a change of unit ``between campaigns''. The images show only the two changes above. Fine-pattern blocks, marker layouts and illumination fields agree on them, and the operators confirmed them against their notes. Campaign D also recorded bare-gel frames (a start frame and lubricant pre/post pairs) and, in seven sessions, a 3-s bare-gel lead-in before every grasp.
\paragraph{Crops.} Material models read twelve native 192-px crops per episode: four start frames inside the stroke window, three fixed positions each. Markers are inpainted per frame. Single-frame models read the first frame of each crop. The eight-frame variant sums eight consecutive frames exactly. Force and contact models read whole native frames.
\paragraph{State fields.} Per session, the position-wise sum and count of its contact crops in frame coordinates, shrunk toward the all-session mean with weight 2 where few crops cover a pixel. A crop's own episode is left out when its own session is the source. The reference $G$ is the mean over the training sessions.
\paragraph{Encoders and heads.} The CNN's input is the BGR crop, two band-pass detail images (the crop minus its 9-px and 33-px box means) and two log colour ratios high-passed at 33\,px (11 channels). Four stages of two $3\times3$ convolutions with BatchNorm and GELU and a blur-pool downsampling (widths 32, 64, 96, 128) are followed by global average pooling and a linear map to 192 dimensions. Training: cross-entropy, 12 epochs, AdamW (lr $3\cdot10^{-4}$, weight decay $10^{-3}$, cosine schedule), batch 48. Sparsh (ViT-B/16) runs on native 192-px crops ($12\times12$ tokens) with its sinusoidal position embeddings regenerated for that grid and its own two-frame input (frames 7 and 2 of an eight-frame crop). Every encoder reads the same native crops, so that all rows of the material table see the same pixels. Sparsh was pretrained on whole frames at a lower resolution with a background frame subtracted, and we did not evaluate it in that protocol. DINOv2-base runs on the central 182\,px of the eight-frame mean with ImageNet normalisation. Features are mean-pooled final tokens (768-d). Heads: MLP (256 hidden, dropout 0.2, 40 epochs, AdamW) on standardised frozen embeddings, three seeds, logits summed per episode.

\section{The tracker}
\paragraph{Settings.} Recent window $K=8$ episodes, reference window $W=30$. Fine statistic: correlation of the 190--370\,\si{\micro\metre} bands (difference of 17-px and 33-px box means of the grey image) of the two window means, over pixels at least 60\,px from every marker of the sessions in the windows. Illumination statistic: mean absolute difference of the two window means after a Gaussian blur with $\sigma=33$\,px. Rule, fixed before evaluation: $\tau_f$ is the 10th percentile and $\tau_i$ the 95th percentile of the statistics over the first hardware state's episodes (on whole frames $\tau_f=\FrTauF$, $\tau_i=\FrTauI$ grey levels). An alarm needs $m=5$ consecutive episodes beyond both; detection then pauses for $W+K$ episodes. Estimate: per-object means with a one-object prior. After an alarm the prior becomes $G$ plus the Gaussian-blurred ($\sigma=33$\,px) difference between the old estimate and $G$. Markers are detected on the first frame of each session's first raw video (\texttt{scripts/build\_session\_dots.py}).
\paragraph{Detector input.} The detector reads the mean of an episode's four whole contact frames (native, markers excluded as above). Our first implementation computed the same statistics on the material model's twelve crops per episode, which cover each frame sparsely, so that an eight-episode window holds one object seen through a few crops per pixel. With the same rule ($\tau_f=\TrkTauF$, $\tau_i=\TrkTauI$) it raised \TrkAlarms\ alarm on the four-month stream, \TrkGelLatency\ episodes after the gel replacement, and missed the change of unit: there the illumination statistic stayed beyond its threshold for \TrkUnitIllumRun\ episodes but the fine statistic for at most \TrkUnitFineRun. On spliced streams it detected \TeOursDet\pct\ of the changes. Reading campaign D with its restarts gave \SrcSupTrkAcc\pct\ for the CNN when run since June, because the estimate for the September sessions still averaged objects of the first unit, and \SrcSupTrkDepAcc\pct\ when started in September. A per-object variant on the crops, which compares each new object's mean field after four episodes with the mean of the other objects since the last change, tested \ObjTested\ objects, raised \ObjAlarms\ alarm, a false one, and caught neither change. Whole frames, the input the detector uses on the public datasets, fixed the problem without changing the rule. This was verified on the stream that had revealed the problem and on spliced streams from the same recordings. Before the percentile rule, an earlier version of the detector chose its thresholds from a scan of 210 settings on our stream. The windows ($K$, $W$), the persistence $m$, the band and the blur come from that version.
\paragraph{Spliced streams.} For each public dataset the same statistics are computed with the band and blur converted to its pixel size, on whole images with dark marker blobs masked. Streams consist of $W+K+10$ items of one hardware state followed by $2W$ items of another state (100 streams) or of other recordings of the same state (100 streams). Thresholds use the same percentiles, computed on one unit of each dataset: our S1, SITR Mini 0, FEATS the training split's main unit, and Tactile MNIST gel 1 in its first three runs. A change counts as detected if an alarm falls within $3W$ items. Items keep their recording order on our data (one object per session) and on Tactile MNIST (runs in order). SITR and FEATS images are in random order, which mixes objects within a window and makes their test easier. Tactile MNIST has one gel swap, tested in both directions. FEATS has one unit with an old and a new gel and a second unit with two gels, and SITR has four Minis. The calibration unit also appears in the test streams. \Cref{tab:roc} shows the trade-off for other percentiles. On the real four-month stream with whole frames, the 1st\,/\,99th percentiles detect the unit and the gel change after \FrSweepOneUnit\ and \FrSweepOneGel\ episodes with \FrSweepOneFalse\ false alarms, the 5th\,/\,95th with \FrSweepFiveFalse, the 10th\,/\,90th with \FrSweepTenFalse\ and the 20th\,/\,80th with \FrSweepTwentyFalse.

\section{Hand-picked state sources}
The previous session's state is the mean contact field of the session recorded just before the test session, whichever object it held. The bare-gel reading uses the marker-inpainted bare-gel frames that campaign D recorded before each session: $\hat F_s = \bar F + (B_s - \bar B)$, where $B_s$ is the session's mean bare-gel frame, $\bar B$ the mean of these over campaign D, and $\bar F$ the mean contact field of campaign D's sessions. A bare-gel frame alone differs from a contact frame by the gel's deformation and lighting under load, so it is placed relative to the contact mean. That mean uses the whole test campaign, which makes the reading non-causal. Campaigns A--C hold no bare-gel frame: their recordings start after the grasp has closed, with \AbcFirstContactPct\pct\ of \AbcEpisodes\ episodes above 0.3\,N in their first frame.

\section{Adaptation baselines}
All baselines use the trained single-frame CNNs with heads fitted on raw training embeddings (three head seeds, three encoder seeds). Label-shift correction: EM re-estimation of the class priors from the episode posteriors of the whole test campaign~\cite{saerens2002labelshift}, applied as a logit offset. AdaBN~\cite{li2018adabn}: BatchNorm statistics re-estimated on the test crops of each hardware state, using the true change date. The causal version uses only the crops of the state seen so far. TENT~\cite{wang2021tent}: entropy minimisation of the BatchNorm affine parameters with batch statistics (Adam, lr $10^{-3}$), reset at the change date. Online, it takes one step per episode in recording order on that episode's twelve crops. With mixed batches, the crops of a hardware state are shuffled into batches of 64 for one pass and then predicted with the adapted parameters, which a robot cannot do.


\section{Corrections without a state estimate}
Three CNNs differ from the plain CNN only in their input or training, with three seeds each and the same 12 epochs. Instance normalisation standardises each input crop per channel over its pixels and maps it to $0.5+0.15z$. The high-pass input is $x-\mathrm{blur}_{\sigma=33\,\mathrm{px}}(x)+0.5$ per channel, which removes the smooth illumination field. Fourier domain adaptation~\cite{fda2020} replaces, with probability 0.5 during training, the low-frequency amplitude spectrum ($\beta=0.01$) of a training crop with that of another crop in the batch. Read with Algorithm~1, their training images are read relative to the same-state reference like every other model.

\section{Variants of the estimate}
All variants share the detector and its alarms. Uniform weighting averages contacts rather than objects. The decay variant multiplies the weight of every earlier object by $\lambda=0.5^{1/2}$ each time a new object begins, including the one-object prior, so that the estimate forgets an object after a few others. Prior $G$ restarts the estimate after an alarm from the training mean alone, without the old smooth illumination. The September variants start the stream at the first session of campaign D, after the change of unit, with and without restarts at later alarms. \section{RoTTA and the ResNet-18 baseline}
RoTTA~\cite{yuan2023rotta} replaces every BatchNorm layer with a robust one that normalises with global statistics, moved with momentum 0.05 by the memory bank's statistics. It keeps a category-balanced memory of 64 test crops, ranked by age and prediction entropy, and predicts with a teacher that is a moving average (0.001) of a student. After every 64 test crops the student's affine BatchNorm parameters take an Adam step (learning rate $10^{-3}$) on a timeliness-weighted cross-entropy between the teacher's prediction and the student's prediction on a strongly augmented copy (photometric jitter and flips). As for TENT, the stream is reset at the true gel replacement, and three encoder seeds and three head seeds are averaged (\texttt{scripts/tta\_rotta.py}). The ResNet-18 starts from torchvision's ImageNet weights, reads the BGR crop as RGB with ImageNet normalisation, and is fine-tuned with the CNN's recipe (12 epochs, AdamW, three seeds). Its rows use the protocol of the material table. With the previous session's state as the training reference instead, Algorithm~1 gives \FoDResTrkPrevAcc\pct.

\Cref{fig:sitrminis} shows that SITR's Minis differ in more than an additive image. The standard deviation of the contact signal differs by a factor of \SmContrastRatio\ between the Minis, and Mini 4 sees a different part of its gel. We repeated the CNN experiment ($k=4$, the same four pairs, one new draw of tied classes) with readings that also equalise these differences. Besides the mean, the location-and-scale reading divides by each Mini's per-pixel standard deviation, and the colour reading aligns each Mini's $3\times3$ colour covariance with the average of the two. The accuracy on the classes learned on the other Mini is \SmStreamCross\pct\ with the mean alone, \SmLocscaleCross\pct\ with location and scale, and \SmColourCross\pct\ with colour alignment. A CNN trained to tell the two Minis apart identifies the Mini in \SmStreamProbe\pct\ of the held-out images after every one of these readings. The field of view is a geometric difference that no per-pixel reading removes, and a CNN trained from scratch on a few thousand images uses it. We measured the same quantity on both datasets with one protocol (\texttt{scripts/unit\_probe\_cnn.py}). A CNN is trained from scratch for six epochs to tell two hardware states apart and tested on objects it has not seen: on our data the \UpOursMaterials\ materials recorded on both unit 1 with gel 1 and unit 2 with gel 2, split in two halves used in turn, and on SITR each of the six pairs of Minis with the 20 objects split in two halves. Raw, it identifies our unit in \UpOursRaw\pct\ of the crops and SITR's Mini in \UpSitrRaw\pct\ of the images. After the reading, our unit falls to \UpOursRead\pct\ and SITR's Mini stays at \UpSitrRead\pct\ for every pair of Minis. On Real Tactile MNIST, with the digits split in two halves, the probe tells gel 0 from gel 1 in \UpTmnistRaw\pct\ of the touches raw and \UpTmnistRead\pct\ after the reading.

\section{Label-free adaptation of a frozen encoder}
Each training crop is re-rendered under another session's state, $x' = x - F_s(p) + F_{s'}(p)$. A small MLP on the frozen Sparsh features (768--768--256) is trained with InfoNCE to give $x$ and $x'$ similar embeddings, and to separate $x$ from a decoy: another crop's residual $x_d - F_{s_d}(p)$ placed under $x$'s own state and position, $F_s(p)$. Batches are drawn from a few sessions so that the other samples in a batch share the anchor's state. The adapter uses no material labels, but it uses each crop's session, which in our data identifies the object. Results on campaign D (three seeds): raw \IjepaAdAcc\pct\ (\DOneIjepaAd\pct\ on the cross-unit materials), with the previous session's state removed \IjepaAdPrevAcc\pct; with photometric-jitter positives instead of the state swap, \IjepaAdJitterAcc\pct\ raw and \IjepaAdJitterPrevAcc\pct\ with the state removed; without decoys \IjepaAdNoDecoyAcc\pct. Combined with the state reading, the adapter adds about a point over the reading alone (\IjepaPrevAcc\pct) and stays below Algorithm~1 under the same protocol, with training crops read relative to their previous session (\IjepaTrkFrAcc\pct). The main paper's material table reads training crops relative to the mean of their hardware state instead, under which Algorithm~1 gives \IjepaTrkStAcc\pct\ for Sparsh-I-JEPA. On Real Tactile MNIST it did not transfer to digit shape.

\section{Force and contact}
\paragraph{Normal force in brief.}
Force is a matter of mechanics, and the state does not describe it. Whole-frame models trained on June and July to regress normal force from appearance reach $R^2\le\ForceLearnedBestR$ on September and October, even with the state removed. Marker displacements against a bare-gel grid recorded in September, fitted on June and July, predict the September and October sessions before the gel replacement at $r=\ForceGeomXeraC$ ($R^2=\ForceGeomXeraR$, supplement).

\begin{figure}[h]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig/fig_force.pdf}
\caption{$R^2$ of log normal force on September--October for models trained on June--July.}
\end{figure}
Marker grids are detected on the bare gel at native resolution (63--64 dots, the Mini's $7\times9$ grid), matched to each contact frame's dots within 0.6 grid spacings, and summarised by the affine fit of the displacement field (divergence, mean displacement, net shear). Within a session the divergence follows the Nano17 force at Spearman \ForceWithinRho. Fitted on the first 13 sessions of campaign D, the linear map predicts the last 7 at $R^2$ \ForceGeomFreshR\ ($r$ \ForceGeomFreshC) with a fresh bare-gel grid and \ForceGeomPrevR\ ($r$ \ForceGeomPrevC) with the previous session's grid. A new gel keeps the force ranking but changes the scale from displacement to force, so absolute force needs one scale calibration per gel. Contact: detectors are trained on the seven lead-in sessions (lead-in and pre-contact frames are no contact, onset and contact frames are contact) and tested on the other sessions' contact frames and bare-gel frames. Referencing the embedding to the session's own bare gel gives \ContactRefSpec\pct\ specificity at \ContactRefRecall\pct\ recall. Thresholded raw differencing against the bare gel gives \ContactBlankdiffSpecMin--\ContactBlankdiffSpecMax\pct\ specificity, depending on the lead-in frames available to fit its threshold.

\section{Measurement details}
The signature uses pixels covered by at least three crops of the partner field and at least 60\,px from every marker of the session. With the marker surroundings included, the same-hardware medians are about 0.05 higher and the cross-hardware floor rises from \HwFarOtherUnit\ to \HwOtherUnit, which is inpainting residue. Partner categories follow the hardware timeline: own session (episode left out), another object the same day, the same unit and gel on another day within a month, the same unit and gel three months apart (July 10--13 against September 28 to October 9), the other unit, and the other gel of the same unit. Real Tactile MNIST: bands of 3--7\,px, its own run (object left out), other runs of the same day, other days of the same gel, and the other gel.
