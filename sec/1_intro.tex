\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A vision-based tactile sensor (VBTS) presses an elastomer gel onto a surface and photographs the deformation under internal LEDs~\cite{yuan2017gelsight,dong2017improved,lambeta2020digit}. Its images are treated as pictures of the contact. They feed encoders that read material, force or slip, foundation models pretrained on millions of such frames~\cite{higuera2024sparsh,t3fota2024,unitouch2024,anytouch2025}, and policies that run on robot hands for months~\cite{suresh2024neuralfeels,qi2023rotateit,helmut2025tactilediffusion,lambeta2024digit360}. Over those months the sensor itself changes. It is powered off and on, its gel is lubricated and worn, and eventually the gel or the whole unit is replaced.

An image is formed by the camera, the LEDs and the gel cartridge as well as by the object. These parts leave a faint pattern of their own that appears in every image, whatever is touched. We call this part of the image the sensor's \emph{hardware state}. \Cref{fig:signature} shows how strong it is. At a fixed image position, the fine detail of a contact correlates at \HwFarOwn\ with other contacts of the same object. It correlates at \HwFarWeeks\ with other objects pressed on the same unit and gel weeks later, and at \HwFarMonths\ three months later. With a different unit or a different gel the correlation drops to \HwFarOtherUnit\ and \HwFarOtherGel. A model that reads touch also reads the hardware, and this paper asks what it does with it.

That the sensor appears in the image is known, and three practices deal with it. The first subtracts a frame of the unloaded gel, captured once or at intervals~\cite{gelslim2018,tactic2026,higuera2024sparsh,sitr2025,anytouch2026two,htt2026}. The second diversifies the training data across units and optics~\cite{suresh2024neuralfeels,tactic2026,sitr2025}. The third avoids raw appearance, for example by reading marker displacements, which survive ``damage or gel replacements''~\cite{rdp2025}. The evaluations behind these practices use one collection, or several units recorded at about the same time~\cite{sitr2025,bideta2026,feats2025,tacverse2026,rct2026,anytouch2026two}, and the longest span we found is three weeks~\cite{tactilemnist2025}. We found no study that follows one unit and gel for months, measures what the blank frame removes, or asks what a change of hardware does to a classifier trained with one object per session, which is how many tactile datasets are recorded. Other imaging fields have asked it, and found classifiers that learn the hospital from an X-ray~\cite{zech2018hospital} or the scanner from an MRI~\cite{fortin2017combat}.

We recorded \EpRecorded\ grasps of 20 materials over four months with two GelSight Mini units and three gels. The images alone show when the hardware changed, and they corrected our laboratory log. The state is also a shortcut. Because every object is recorded in its own session, a classifier can satisfy its labels with the hardware state. A CNN trained on June and July, when unit 2 had been used for only four materials, assigns \SrcSupNoneTop\pct\ of the September and October episodes to one of those four materials. It recognises the fifteen materials it learned on the first unit at \DOneSupRaw\pct, where chance is 5\pct, and frozen Sparsh encoders~\cite{higuera2024sparsh} collapse in the same way. Our explanation is that a classifier trained with one object per session learns each unit as a feature of the classes recorded on it, so that under the new unit's state everything looks like those classes. We test this explanation under control. SITR~\cite{sitr2025} pressed the same 20 objects on four GelSight Minis. When we tie a single object to one Mini during training, accuracy on all other objects tested on that Mini falls from \CfSitrRawZeroCross\ to \CfSitrRawOneCross\pct, and \CfSitrRawOneTop\pct\ of the test images go to the tied object.

The cure is to break the tie between class and state, and we study two label-free ways to do it. During training, each image can be re-rendered under the state of another session, so that every class is seen under several states. During deployment, the robot keeps a running estimate of the state from its contacts with different objects, the retrospective flat-field of microscopy~\cite{singh2014illumination,peng2017basic}, restarts it when a detector sees new hardware, and reads each image relative to it (\cref{alg:tracker}). The reading raises the CNN from \SrcSupNoneAcc\ to \FoDSupTrkStateAcc\pct\ and frozen Sparsh-I-JEPA from \IjepaRawAcc\ to \IjepaTrkStAcc\pct, while test-time adaptation reaches at most \TtaSupAdabnEpochsAcc\pct. It does as well as a bare-gel frame of each session, which our first campaigns never recorded. On a reversed split whose predictions we wrote down before training, the collapse and its repair appeared as predicted. The tools repair the shortcut, but not everything a change of hardware costs. A CNN trained on June alone, with one session per material, loses about 30 points on the other unit, with or without the reading. Where two sensors differ in more than an additive image, as SITR's Minis differ in their field of view, a CNN trained from scratch still recognises the sensor after the reading, and it still collapses.

Our contributions are:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
  \item A four-month measurement of the hardware state of a VBTS. The state is present in the bare gel, stable over months of use, and replaced with the unit or the gel, so a dataset's images record its hardware history.
  \item An account of how classifiers trained with one object per session fail after a change of hardware, confirmed by injecting the same structure into a public dataset. The classes recorded under the new state absorb the test set.
  \item Two label-free tools that break the tie between class and state: state augmentation during training, and a tracker that estimates the state from the robot's own contacts, detects hardware changes, and reads each image relative to the estimate. We test them with supervised and frozen foundation encoders on every campaign of our data, in a reversed split fixed before training, and on three public datasets, and we show where they stop working.
  \item A four-month VBTS dataset with two units, three gels, 20 materials, co-recorded force, bare-gel frames and a leave-one-campaign-out protocol, which we release with the code.
\end{itemize}
