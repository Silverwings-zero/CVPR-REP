\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A vision-based tactile sensor (VBTS) presses an elastomer gel onto a surface and photographs the deformation under internal LEDs~\cite{yuan2017gelsight,dong2017improved,lambeta2020digit}. The frame is treated as a picture of the contact. It is fed to encoders that read material, force or slip, to foundation models pretrained on millions of such frames~\cite{higuera2024sparsh,t3fota2024,unitouch2024,anytouch2025}, and to policies that run on robot hands for months: DIGIT fingertips track objects in hand~\cite{suresh2024neuralfeels}, multi-fingered hands rotate unseen objects~\cite{qi2023rotateit}, a GelSight Mini conditions a manipulation policy on force~\cite{helmut2025tactilediffusion}, and a multimodal fingertip is sold as a product~\cite{lambeta2024digit360}. Over those months the sensor changes. It is powered off and on, its gel is lubricated, worn and eventually replaced, and the rig around it is rebuilt.

That the sensor is in the image is known, and three practices deal with it. The first subtracts a blank frame of the unloaded gel, captured at the start of an episode or periodically: GelSlim re-captures it every hundred grasps~\cite{gelslim2018}, TACTIC averages fifty frames per rollout~\cite{tactic2026}, and Sparsh, SITR, AnyTouch~2 and HTT subtract one as preprocessing~\cite{higuera2024sparsh,sitr2025,anytouch2026two,htt2026}. The second diversifies the training data: NeuralFeels renders with the backgrounds of 25 DIGITs and random LEDs~\cite{suresh2024neuralfeels}, TACTIC records with four cartridges, SITR simulates a hundred optical configurations and calibrates each unit with eighteen presses~\cite{sitr2025}. The third avoids the image: RotateIt reduces it to a contact location~\cite{qi2023rotateit}, and a reactive diffusion policy reads marker displacements because they survive ``damage or gel replacements''~\cite{rdp2025}. None of these measures what the blank removes, and none tests the same unit months later. Every published evaluation is within a collection; the closest proxies swap units~\cite{sitr2025,bideta2026,feats2025,tacverse2026,rct2026} or gels~\cite{anytouch2026two}, or span two weeks~\cite{tactilemnist2025}. Other imaging fields have asked the question: classifiers learn the hospital from an X-ray~\cite{zech2018hospital}, the scanner from an MRI~\cite{fortin2017combat}, the dataset from a photograph~\cite{torralba2011bias,liuhe2025}, and microscopy estimates its flat-field from the images themselves when no blank exists~\cite{singh2014illumination,peng2017basic}. For touch, nobody has.

We recorded one GelSight Mini for four months: 2{,}711 grasps of 20 materials in four campaigns, with the normal force measured on the opposing finger and, in the last campaign, bare-gel frames between grasps. A probe with no model shows the sensor in the image (\cref{fig:signature}). At a fixed image position, the fine-scale content of a contact correlates with the mean of other contacts at \SigCOwn\ for its own session, \SigCSameDay\ for another object touched the same day, \SigSameEraOtherDay\ for another day of the same campaign and \SigCOtherEra\ for another campaign. The pattern is in the bare gel (\BlankVsField), stays fixed in image coordinates while the membrane moves, and is shared most by thick soft samples that cannot transmit a backing: it is the rendering state of the illumination, optics and camera, not the object. Its consequence is a shortcut. Each object is recorded in one session, so a material classifier can satisfy its labels with the session's state, and does. A CNN trained on three campaigns recognises unseen sessions of a known state at \FoldBSupRawAcc--\FoldASupRawAcc\pct, but the materials of the next campaign, recorded after the rig was rebuilt, at \SupFoneRawSAcc\pct\ (chance 5\pct), with \SupFoneRawSTop\pct\ of all episodes in one class; frozen Sparsh encoders score \SdinoRawAcc--\IjepaRawAcc\pct. That class is a material recorded in a session of its own in the last training campaign, whose illumination state the new campaign resembles most (\StateDistDtoC\ grey levels from its mean field, against \StateDistDtoA\ and \StateDistDtoB\ from the other two). We propose the following interpretation: under an unseen state, everything looks like the classes that a single state identifies.

If that is right, removing the state should undo the collapse, and estimating it should not need a blank frame: the position-wise mean of many contacts of different objects contains the sensor and not the objects, the retrospective flat-field of microscopy~\cite{singh2014illumination,peng2017basic}. It does. Subtracting the previous object's mean field restores the CNN to \SupFonePrevSAcc\pct\ and frozen Sparsh to \IjepaPrevAcc\pct; a bare-gel frame gives \SrcSupBlankAcc\pct, the deployment's own mean \SrcSupCampAcc\pct, and per-session AdaBN \SupFoneAdaSessAcc\pct. The same re-rendering trains a label-free adapter: contacts rendered under other sessions' states as positives, other surfaces under the same state as decoys, which lifts frozen Sparsh to \IjepaAdAcc\pct\ with no target data, against \IjepaAdJitterAcc\pct\ with photometric positives. Holding out each campaign in turn bounds the claim. The correction matters only for an unseen state, is unnecessary within known states, and cannot cross a gel replacement, which changes mechanics; normal force then has to be read from marker geometry against the unloaded gel ($r$=\ForceGeomXeraC\ across months) where appearance fails ($R^2\le\ForceLearnedBestR$). The pattern and the collapse replicate on another lab's GelSight Mini~\cite{tactilemnist2025}.

Our contributions:
\begin{itemize}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
  \item We show that a VBTS frame carries its campaign's rendering state: present in the bare gel, fixed in image coordinates, persisting for weeks (\SigSameEraOtherDay) and breaking at a rebuild (\SigCOtherEra).
  \item We trace the resulting collapse (\SupFoneRawSAcc\pct\ after months, \FoldBSupRawAcc--\FoldASupRawAcc\pct\ within the state) to the classes that a single session's state identifies, and to the proximity of states.
  \item We show that a retrospective estimate from the contact stream removes the state as well as a blank does (\SupFonePrevSAcc--\SrcSupBlankAcc\pct), where normalisation statistics fail, and that re-rendering under it adapts frozen encoders without labels (\IjepaAdAcc\pct).
  \item We release a four-month, same-unit VBTS dataset: 2{,}711 grasps, 20 materials, co-recorded force, bare-gel frames, power cycles and a gel replacement, with the leave-one-campaign-out protocol.
\end{itemize}
