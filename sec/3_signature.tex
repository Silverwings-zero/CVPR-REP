\section{The day signature}
\label{sec:signature}

\paragraph{Data.}
A GelSight Mini (1640$\times$1232 native frames, \SI{11.3}{\micro\metre}/px, \SI{18.8}{fps}, printed markers) on one finger of a parallel gripper; an ATI Nano17 on the other finger records the normal force. Each episode is a grasp of one of 20 materials at a commanded force (0.9--5\,N) followed by a short stroke; the gel imprint is static during the stroke (phase-correlation shift 0.1--0.6\,px against 21--108\,px expected from the end-effector motion). Four campaigns: A (June 15--22, 570 episodes), B (July 6--8, 450), C (July 9--13, 340) and D (September 22 -- October 15, 1{,}351); every session records one material; the gel was replaced on October 9 inside campaign D. Campaign D also records bare-gel frames (620 in 44 sessions) and, in seven sessions, a bare-gel lead-in before every grasp. We never resample a frame: material models read native 192-px crops, force and contact models read whole native frames.

\paragraph{Measurement.}
For every session we form the \emph{state field} $F_s(p)$: the mean of its contact crops at each image position $p$ (markers inpainted). For sampled contacts we correlate the band-passed crop (190--370\,\si{\micro\metre}; markers masked) with the band-passed field of (a) its own session with its episode left out, (b) another session recorded the same day with a different material, (c) a session of another campaign on the same gel, (d) a session on the other gel. Medians over sessions: \SigOwn, \SigSameDay, \SigOtherEra, \SigOtherGel\ (\cref{fig:signature}). The same-day value is close to the own-session value although the material differs, and the other-day value is close to the other-gel value: most of the stable fine-scale content of a contact image is set by the sensor on that day. The pattern is broadband (video-codec row harmonics hold 3\pct\ of its power), is strongest on rigid samples (same-day \num{0.62} for wood, ceramic, ABS, fibreglass, cork), so it is not a print-through of the sample's backing, and it does not appear in the bare gel: a session's blank frame correlates at \num{0.06} with its own contact field. Flat-fielding by a blank frame, the standard per-session reference, therefore leaves it intact (we verified: no gain).

\paragraph{Drift mechanisms.}
From the bare-gel frames, consecutive blanks bracket three kinds of interval. Use (a median of five grasps between blanks) changes the low-frequency illumination field by \num{0.13} grey levels while the pad's fine pattern stays at the metric floor; a session restart with no contact changes it by \num{0.40} (camera and LED re-initialisation); the gel replacement reduces the pattern correlation to \num{0.67} and, as \cref{sec:force} shows, changes the gel's mechanics. On the first gel the cumulative illumination drift tracks contacts, not hours (partial $r$ \num{0.39} vs \num{-0.20}).

\paragraph{It is the shortcut.}
We train an era classifier (June vs July) on contacts of the 15 materials recorded in both, so material cannot predict the era (balanced accuracy \num{76.6}\pct\ on held-out episodes). June contacts re-rendered under a July session's state---$x - F_{\text{June}}(p) + F_{\text{July}}(p)$---are classified as July \RerenderAtoB\pct\ of the time, against \RerenderRealA\pct\ for real June contacts, \RerenderAtoA\pct\ for June contacts re-rendered under another June state, and \RerenderRealB\pct\ for real July contacts. The state field carries most of the day cue.

\paragraph{A second unit.}
Real Tactile MNIST~\cite{tactilemnist2025} records another lab's GelSight Mini touching printed digits in 24 runs over 15 days with one gel swap. With the same measurement on its 320$\times$240 images, a touch matches other runs of the same day at \TmSigSameDay, other days at \TmSigOtherDay\ and the other gel at \TmSigOtherGel\ (\cref{fig:signature}).

\paragraph{Integration feeds the shortcut.}
Averaging consecutive native frames of the static imprint raises the reliability of fine texture (34--100\,\si{\micro\metre}: \num{0.14} for one frame, \num{0.48} for eight)---but the content it stabilises is mostly the day signature. We return to this in \cref{sec:ablation}: cross-day accuracy after state removal is highest with single frames.
