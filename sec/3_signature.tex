\section{A tactile image is the touch rendered through the sensor's state}
\label{sec:signature}

\begin{figure*}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig/fig_state.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{The sensor's state over four months.} Left: distance of each session's loaded-state field (low-frequency illumination and colour) from the June--July mean, by date; the state drifts within and between campaigns and jumps at the gel replacement. Right: change of the unloaded gel between consecutive bare-gel frames, by what happened in between: lubricant alone barely changes it, five grasps change it a little, a power cycle with no contact changes it three times as much (camera and LED re-initialisation).}
  \label{fig:state}
\end{figure*}

We write a contact image at position $p$ in session $s$ as $x = F_s(p) + r$: the sensor's state at that position in that session, plus the touch. This section shows that $F_s$ is a real, measurable quantity: it is shared by every object touched in the same campaign, it is present in the bare gel and fixed in image coordinates, and swapping it moves a contact to another campaign.

\paragraph{Data.}
A GelSight Mini (1640$\times$1232 native frames, \SI{11.3}{\micro\metre}/px, \SI{18.8}{fps}, printed markers) on one finger of a parallel gripper; an ATI Nano17 on the other finger records the normal force. Each episode is a grasp of one of 20 materials at a commanded force (0.9--5\,N) followed by a short stroke; the gel imprint is static during the stroke (phase-correlation shift 0.1--0.6\,px against 21--108\,px expected from the end-effector motion). Four campaigns: A (June 15--22, 570 episodes), B (July 6--8, 450), C (July 9--13, 340) and D (September 22 -- October 15, 1{,}351); every session records one material; the gel was replaced on October 9 inside campaign D. Campaign D also records bare-gel frames (620 in 44 sessions) and, in seven sessions, a bare-gel lead-in before every grasp. We never resample a frame: material models read native 192-px crops, force and contact models read whole native frames.

\paragraph{The state is shared across objects, for weeks.}
For every session we form the \emph{state field} $F_s(p)$: the mean of its contact crops at each image position $p$ (markers inpainted). For sampled contacts we correlate the band-passed crop (190--370\,\si{\micro\metre}; markers masked) with the band-passed field of (a) its own session with its episode left out, (b) another session of the same day with a different material, (c) a session of the same campaign on another day, (d) a session of another campaign on the same gel, (e) a session on the other gel; all partner pairs. Medians over sessions: \SigOwn, \SigSameDay, \SigSameEraOtherDay, \SigOtherEra, \SigOtherGel\ (\cref{fig:signature}). A different object touched the same day, or days later in the same campaign, shares almost as much of the fine-scale content as the same session's other contacts; another campaign shares little more than the other gel. The state is a property of a recording campaign (weeks), not of a day.

\paragraph{It is the sensor, not the object or the fixture.}
Three facts locate the pattern. It is present with nothing on the gel: for the seven sessions with bare-gel lead-ins, the bare-gel frame processed like the contact crops correlates with the session's contact field at \num{0.64}, more than the loaded frames themselves (\num{0.50}). It is fixed in image coordinates: warping the bare-gel frame by the measured membrane displacement \emph{raises} its match to the same episode's contact frame (\num{0.18}$\rightarrow$\num{0.40} where the membrane moved more than 8\,px) but \emph{lowers} its match to the session field, whose average over many contacts keeps only the image-fixed part---so the shared pattern is in the illumination, the optics and the camera, not on the moving membrane. And on the training gel it is shared most by thick compliant samples (foams and fleece, same-day \num{0.54}) that cannot transmit a fixture face, and least by rigid ones (\num{0.33}) whose own texture dominates. Flat-fielding by a blank frame, the standard per-session reference, is therefore a valid estimate of this state where blanks exist; \cref{sec:method} shows that the contact stream gives the same estimate where they do not.

\paragraph{How the state changes (\cref{fig:state}).}
The low-frequency illumination field, read from the bare-gel frames, moves by \num{0.13} grey levels over five grasps and by \num{0.40} at a session restart with no contact (camera and LED re-initialisation), and on the first gel its cumulative drift tracks contacts rather than hours (partial $r$ \num{0.39} vs \num{-0.20}); the fine-scale pattern, in contrast, is stable within a campaign and breaks between campaigns (our rig was rebuilt between July and September) and at the gel replacement (pattern correlation \num{0.67}).

\paragraph{Swapping the state moves a contact to another campaign.}
We train an era classifier (June vs July) on contacts of the 15 materials recorded in both, so material cannot predict the era (balanced accuracy \num{76.6}\pct\ on held-out episodes). June contacts re-rendered under a July session's state---$x - F_{\text{June}}(p) + F_{\text{July}}(p)$---are classified as July \RerenderAtoB\pct\ of the time, against \RerenderRealA\pct\ for real June contacts, \RerenderAtoA\pct\ for June contacts re-rendered under another June state, and \RerenderRealB\pct\ for real July contacts. The state field carries most of the campaign cue.

\paragraph{A second unit.}
Real Tactile MNIST~\cite{tactilemnist2025} records another lab's GelSight Mini touching printed digits in 24 runs over 15 days with one gel swap. With the same measurement on its 320$\times$240 images, a touch matches other runs of the same day at \TmSigSameDay, other days of the same three-week campaign at \TmSigOtherDay\ and the other gel at \TmSigOtherGel\ (\cref{fig:signature}): the same ordering, with the gel as the large step.

\paragraph{Integration feeds the shortcut.}
Averaging consecutive native frames of the static imprint raises the reliability of fine texture (34--100\,\si{\micro\metre}: \num{0.14} for one frame, \num{0.48} for eight)---but the content it stabilises is mostly the sensor's state, including its session-level part (own session \SigCOwn\ vs other sessions of the campaign \SigSameEraOtherDay). We return to this in \cref{sec:ablation}: accuracy on new sessions, within or across campaigns, is highest with single frames.
