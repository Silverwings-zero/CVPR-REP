\section{The hardware state in a tactile image}
\label{sec:signature}

We first characterize the image structure associated with the sensor hardware and examine how it changes with use and hardware replacement. \Cref{sec:shortcut} then tests whether classifiers exploit this structure when material classes are associated with particular units or gels.

\paragraph{Recording setup.}
A GelSight Mini (1640$\times$1232 native frames, \SI{11.3}{\micro\metre}/px, \SI{18.8}{fps}) is mounted on one finger of a two-finger parallel gripper on a 7-DoF Franka Emika Panda arm, and an ATI Nano17 force-torque sensor on the other finger measures the normal force (\cref{fig:setup}). We use the gel with printed markers because their displacement measures shear and slip~\cite{yuan2017gelsight,dong2017improved}, which manipulation needs. The object is a box, held in a fixture, whose face carries one of 20 materials, all but fiberglass among the textures of the Penn Haptic Texture Toolkit (HaTT)~\cite{culbertson2014hatt}: ABS plastic, aluminum foil, a bubble envelope, canvas, cardboard, ceramic, cork, EPDM foam, fiberglass, fleece, glitter paper, the two faces of a piece of leather, pleather, polyethylene foam, r\'esum\'e paper, silk, textured rubber, vinyl, and wood (\supp{A} shows a photograph of each and a contact with it).

\paragraph{Motion.}
In each episode the gripper closes on the box in small steps until the Nano17 reads a target force, then holds its width. The arm, under Cartesian impedance control, slides the gel along the face in steps of 1 to 2\,mm. It strokes 2\,cm to each side and 2\,cm up and down, returns to the center after each stroke, then follows a circle of 2\,cm radius (\cref{fig:setup}). The target force is 40 or 80\pct\ of the largest force in the material's HaTT model, between 0.5 and 5\,N, and the strokes reach 9 to 29\,mm/s (\supp{A}). Every five episodes an operator re-lubricates the gel with petroleum jelly (Vaseline), as dry sliding can damage it.

\paragraph{Campaigns and hardware.}
One session records one material. Four data collection campaigns cover four months: A (June 15--22), B (July 6--8), C (July 9--13) and D (September 22 to October 15), \EpRecorded\ episodes in all, of which the material experiments use \EpCrops\ (\supp{A}). Campaign D also records bare-gel frames. The camera's exposure and white balance are fixed, and frames are used at native resolution. Two units and three gels were used, in three combinations, the hardware configurations S1, S2 and S3 (\cref{fig:setup}).

\paragraph{Measurement.}
For every session we form the position-wise mean of its contact crops, its \emph{mean contact frame} $M_s(p)$. We correlate the fine band (190--370\,\si{\micro\metre}) of a sampled contact crop with the same band of another session's mean frame, excluding pixels within 60\,px of a printed marker so that neither the markers nor their inpainting can contribute. \Cref{fig:signature} shows the medians over sessions. The correlation is \HwFarOwn\ with other contacts of the same session, which include the same object, and \HwFarSameDay\ with another object touched the same day. It is \HwFarWeeks\ with the same unit and gel on another day and \HwFarMonths\ three months later, but only \HwFarOtherUnit\ with the other unit and \HwFarOtherGel\ with the other gel of the same unit. Bare-gel frames correlate with their session's mean contact frame at \BlankVsFieldFar. Another lab's GelSight Mini shows the same ordering (orange in \cref{fig:signature}).

\paragraph{Origin and stability of the state.}
\Cref{fig:stateimages} shows the state's two components, a fine spatial pattern and a smooth illumination field. Two tests point to the origin of the fine pattern. Warping a bare-gel frame by the measured membrane displacement lowers its correlation with the session's mean contact frame from \BlankVsFieldFar\ to \WarpedBlankVsFieldFar, so the pattern stays fixed in the camera's view, although the median displacement of only \WarpDispPx\,px makes this test weak. Replacing the gel on the same unit removes the pattern, which links it to the gel cartridge and is consistent with a non-uniform coating.
The smooth component varies modestly between sessions on the same hardware. Each session's illumination field differs from its configuration's mean by \LpDistWithinSthree--\LpDistWithinSone\ gray levels, and S2's field moved by \LpDistStwoJulSep\ over the summer break, against \LpDistSoneStwo\ between the two units and \LpDistStwoSthree\ between the two gels on unit 2. It drifts more while the sensor is idle between sessions than during use (\supp{W}).
