\section{The hardware state in a tactile image}
\label{sec:signature}

\paragraph{Data.}
A GelSight Mini (1640$\times$1232 native frames, \SI{11.3}{\micro\metre}/px, \SI{18.8}{fps}, printed markers) is mounted on one finger of a parallel gripper, and an ATI Nano17 on the other finger measures the normal force. The object is a box whose face carries one of 20 materials: ABS plastic, aluminium foil, a bubble envelope, canvas, cardboard, ceramic, cork, EPDM foam, fibreglass, fleece, glitter paper, the two faces of a piece of leather, pleather, polyethylene foam, r\'esum\'e paper, silk, textured rubber, vinyl and wood. Each episode grasps the face at one of two forces (40 or 80\pct\ of a per-material maximum, 0.9--5\,N) and strokes it by 2\,cm in four directions and a circle. The gel is lubricated every five episodes, and one session records one material. Four campaigns cover four months: A (June 15--22), B (July 6--8), C (July 9--13) and D (September 22 to October 15), \EpRecorded\ episodes in all. \EpNoCrops\ episodes of campaign D have no eight-frame span of contact inside the stroke, so the material experiments use \EpCrops. Campaign D also records frames of the bare gel. The camera's exposure and white balance are fixed (on a held contact the mean of each colour channel drifts by at most 0.3 grey levels per episode), and we never resample a frame.

Two units and three gels were used. Unit 1 carried gel 1 until July 9, unit 2 carried gel 2 from July 10, and gel 3 replaced gel 2 on October 9 between 18:42 and 19:37. We call these three combinations of unit and gel the hardware configurations S1, S2 and S3.

\paragraph{Measurement.}
For every session we form the position-wise mean of its contact crops in frame coordinates, its \emph{mean contact frame} $M_s(p)$. For a sampled contact crop we correlate its fine band (190--370\,\si{\micro\metre}) with the same band of another session's mean frame, excluding pixels within 60\,px of a printed marker so that inpainting residue cannot contribute. \Cref{fig:signature} shows the medians over sessions. A contact matches the other contacts of its own session, which include the same object, at \HwFarOwn. It matches another object touched the same day at \HwFarSameDay, the same unit and gel on another day at \HwFarWeeks, and the same unit and gel three months later at \HwFarMonths. It does not match the other unit (\HwFarOtherUnit) or the other gel of the same unit (\HwFarOtherGel). Frames of the bare gel, with nothing touching it, match their session's mean contact frame at \BlankVsFieldFar. Another lab's GelSight Mini shows the same ordering (orange in \cref{fig:signature}).

\paragraph{What the state is.}
\Cref{fig:stateimages} shows the two parts of the state. Two tests locate the fine pattern. It does not move with the membrane: warping a bare-gel frame by the membrane's measured displacement lowers its match to the session's mean frame from \BlankVsFieldFar\ to \WarpedBlankVsFieldFar. The membrane moves by a median of only \WarpDispPx\,px, however, so this test is weak. The pattern does belong to the gel cartridge, because a new gel on the same unit and camera removes it. We therefore take it to belong to the cartridge and to be fixed in the camera's view, rather than to lie on the deforming surface. A non-uniform coating would behave this way. The smooth part, the illumination field, changes a little from session to session on the same hardware. Between two bare-gel frames of one session, the illumination moves by a median \DriftUse\ grey levels over \DriftUseContacts\ grasps, and by \DriftIdle\ between the last bare-gel frame of one session and the first of the next, with no contact in between, so it moves more while the sensor rests than while it is used. Within one configuration, a session's illumination field lies \LpDistWithinSthree--\LpDistWithinSone\ grey levels from the configuration's mean. The two units differ by \LpDistSoneStwo\ grey levels, and the two gels on unit 2 by \LpDistStwoSthree.

\paragraph{The images record the hardware history.}
The changes are sharp enough to date from the images alone. Correlating every pair of the 35 June and July sessions splits them into two blocks at July 9 to 10 and nowhere else, and the September and October sessions split at October 9. Our laboratory log had recorded a change of unit and four gel replacements. The images show only the two changes above. The operators confirmed these two against their notes, and the other two logged replacements left no trace in the images, so the timeline above is the one the images give. Because the log cannot serve as an independent reference, \cref{sec:trackerexp} tests the detector of \cref{sec:tracker}, which turns the same observation into a test that runs while the robot works, on spliced streams whose change points are known by construction.

\paragraph{The state as a shortcut.}
\label{sec:confound}
In our training data each material appears under one hardware configuration, because each was recorded in its own session, so a classifier can use the state as a feature of the materials recorded under it. The introduction tests this by tying classes to one of SITR's four Minis~\cite{sitr2025}, and \cref{fig:confound} shows the test for every number of tied classes. On Real Tactile MNIST~\cite{tactilemnist2025} we tie digits to gels instead, and tying one digit to the test gel sends \CfTmdRawOneTop\pct\ of the test touches to that digit.
