\section{The hardware state in a tactile image}
\label{sec:signature}

We first characterize the image structure associated with the sensor hardware and examine how it changes with use and hardware replacement. We then test whether classifiers exploit this structure when material classes are associated with particular units or gels.

\paragraph{Recording setup.}
A GelSight Mini (1640$\times$1232 native frames, \SI{11.3}{\micro\metre}/px, \SI{18.8}{fps}) is mounted on one finger of a two-finger parallel gripper on a 7-DoF Franka Emika Panda arm, and an ATI Nano17 force-torque sensor on the other finger measures the normal force (\cref{fig:setup}). We use the gel with printed markers because manipulation needs them: their displacement measures shear and slip~\cite{yuan2017gelsight,dong2017improved}. The object is a box, held in a fixture, whose face carries one of 20 materials drawn from the Penn Haptic Texture Toolkit (HaTT)~\cite{culbertson2014hatt}, from silk and fleece to ceramic and wood (the supplement lists them and shows a contact with each).

\paragraph{Motion.}
In each episode the gripper closes on the box in small steps until the Nano17 reads a target force, then holds its width. The arm, under Cartesian impedance control, slides the gel along the face in steps of 1 to 2\,mm. It strokes 2\,cm to each side and 2\,cm up and down, returns to the center after each stroke, then follows a circle of 2\,cm radius (\cref{fig:setup}). The target force is 40 or 80\pct\ of the largest force in the material's HaTT model, between 0.5 and 5\,N, and the strokes reach 9 to 29\,mm/s (supplement). Every five episodes an operator re-lubricates the gel with petroleum jelly (Vaseline), as dry sliding on some materials damages it.

\paragraph{Campaigns and hardware.}
One session records one material. Four data collection campaigns cover four months: A (June 15--22), B (July 6--8), C (July 9--13) and D (September 22 to October 15), \EpRecorded\ episodes in all, of which the material experiments use \EpCrops\ (supplement). Campaign D also records frames of the bare gel. The camera's exposure and white balance are fixed, and we never resample a frame. Two units and three gels were used, in the three combinations that we call hardware configurations S1, S2 and S3 (\cref{fig:setup}).

\paragraph{Measurement.}
For every session we form the position-wise mean of its contact crops in frame coordinates, its \emph{mean contact frame} $M_s(p)$. We correlate the fine band (190--370\,\si{\micro\metre}) of a sampled contact crop with the same band of another session's mean frame, excluding pixels within 60\,px of a printed marker so that neither the markers nor their inpainting can contribute. \Cref{fig:signature} shows the medians over sessions. A contact matches the other contacts of its own session, which include the same object, at \HwFarOwn. It matches another object touched the same day at \HwFarSameDay, the same unit and gel on another day at \HwFarWeeks, and three months later at \HwFarMonths. It does not match the other unit (\HwFarOtherUnit) or the other gel of the same unit (\HwFarOtherGel). Frames of the bare gel match their session's mean contact frame at \BlankVsFieldFar. Another lab's GelSight Mini shows the same ordering (orange in \cref{fig:signature}).

\paragraph{What the state is.}
\Cref{fig:stateimages} shows the state's two components: a fine spatial pattern and a smooth illumination field. We use two tests to investigate the origin of the fine pattern. First, warping a bare-gel frame by the measured membrane displacement reduces its correlation with the session's mean contact frame from \BlankVsFieldFar\ to \WarpedBlankVsFieldFar. This suggests that the pattern remains fixed in the camera's view rather than moving with the membrane, although the small median displacement of \WarpDispPx\,px limits the strength of this evidence. Second, replacing the gel while retaining the same unit and camera removes the pattern, linking it to the gel cartridge. Together, these observations suggest a cartridge-associated pattern that remains approximately fixed in image coordinates, consistent with a non-uniform coating.
The smooth component varies modestly between sessions on the same hardware. Within a session, the illumination field changes by a median of \DriftUse\ gray levels over \DriftUseContacts\ grasps. Between the last bare-gel frame of one session and the first of the next, it changes by \DriftIdle\ gray levels despite no intervening contact. The larger between-session change shows that the field can vary while the sensor is idle, rather than only through use. Within a hardware configuration, each session's illumination field differs from the configuration mean by \LpDistWithinSthree--\LpDistWithinSone\ gray levels. Differences between configurations are larger: \LpDistSoneStwo\ gray levels between the two units and \LpDistStwoSthree\ between the two gels on unit 2.

\paragraph{The images record the hardware history.}
Changes in the hardware signature are abrupt enough to locate from the images alone. Pairwise correlations among the 35 June and July sessions reveal two distinct blocks, separated between July 9 and 10, with no other clear transition. The September and October sessions show a second transition on October 9. Our laboratory log records one unit change and four gel replacements, but only these two transitions are evident in the images. Operators corroborated both transitions using their notes; the remaining logged changes have no detectable image signature. We therefore use the image-derived timeline for our analysis. Because the laboratory log does not provide a reliable independent reference, we evaluate the online change detector described in \cref{sec:tracker} on spliced recording streams with change points known by construction (\cref{sec:trackerexp}).

\paragraph{The state as a shortcut.}
\label{sec:confound}
In our training data each material appears under one hardware configuration, so a classifier can use the state as a feature of the materials recorded under it. The introduction tests this by tying classes to one of SITR's four Minis~\cite{sitr2025}, and \cref{fig:confound} shows the test for every number of tied classes. On Real Tactile MNIST~\cite{tactilemnist2025}, tying one digit to the test gel sends \CfTmdRawOneTop\pct\ of the test touches to that digit.
