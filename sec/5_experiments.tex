\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\paragraph{Protocol.}
The main split trains on campaigns A, B and C (June and July, \EpTrainABC\ episodes) and tests on campaign D (September and October, \EpTestD\ episodes, 20 materials, chance 5\pct). The test set holds three hardware relations. Fifteen materials were learned on unit 1 and are tested on unit 2, a different unit and gel. Four rigid materials (cork, fibreglass, ABS, ceramic) were learned on unit 2 in July and are tested on its replaced gel. Wood was learned on unit 1 and is tested on the replaced gel of unit 2. Every input is a single native 192-px crop, twelve per episode, and the logits of an episode's crops are summed. We report macro accuracy, macro-F1, and the share of test episodes assigned to the most predicted class, which exposes a collapse onto few classes. The CNN is a four-stage convolutional encoder at native resolution. Its input is the colour crop plus two band-pass detail images and two high-passed log colour ratios, each stage applies two $3\times3$ convolutions with BatchNorm and GELU followed by anti-aliased downsampling, and it is trained with cross-entropy (12 epochs, AdamW). Its jitter variant adds per-channel gains ($\pm$20\pct) and a smooth multiplicative field ($\pm$15\pct) during training. Heads are MLPs on frozen embeddings with three seeds, and CNN numbers average three training seeds. Rows that subtract a state estimate read the training images relative to their own previous session, so that the head sees the same transform at training and test time. All other rows read the training images raw. Sparsh~\cite{higuera2024sparsh} receives its own two-frame input (two frames of the crop, five apart) and DINOv2~\cite{oquab2024dinov2} the mean of eight consecutive frames. All design choices were made on this split, and the other three campaign folds in \cref{sec:timeorstate} are independent of them.

\subsection{What a change of hardware does to a classifier}
\label{sec:material}

\Cref{tab:material} gives the main result. Read raw, the CNN scores \SrcSupNoneAcc\pct\ and puts \SrcSupNoneTop\pct\ of all test episodes into a single class, one of the four materials it saw on unit 2. The fifteen materials it learned on unit 1 score \DOneSupRaw\pct. The four materials learned on unit 2 score \DTwoSupRaw\pct, but only because the classifier sends most episodes to them. Frozen Sparsh encoders show the same collapse. The jitter CNN collapses less (\SrcAugNoneAcc\pct, \SrcAugNoneTop\pct\ in one class) but still scores \DOneAugRaw\pct\ on the cross-unit materials.

Reading the images relative to the state undoes the collapse. With \cref{alg:tracker}, the CNN reaches \SrcSupTrkFrAcc\pct\ and the cross-unit materials \DOneSupTrkFr\pct. This lies between what the previous session's state (\SrcSupPrevAcc\pct) and a bare-gel frame of the same session (\SrcSupBlankAcc\pct) give, although the tracker chooses its own source and restarts by itself, while those two rows are picked by hand. Frozen Sparsh-I-JEPA rises from \IjepaRawAcc\ to \IjepaTrkFrAcc\pct, and the other Sparsh encoders gain as well. The four materials across the gel replacement do not gain. Their raw score is inflated by the collapse, and with the state removed they score \DTwoSupTrkFr\pct\ for the CNN and \DTwoAugTrkFr\pct\ for the jitter CNN (\cref{sec:timeorstate}).

The adaptation baselines do not fix the collapse. Label-shift correction re-estimates the class priors from the test predictions, finds the absorbing class frequent, and makes the collapse worse. Online TENT, which updates on one object at a time as a robot would, collapses entirely, and AdaBN per hardware state reaches \TtaSupAdabnEpochsAcc\pct\ even with the true change dates and all of the state's test images. Both methods assume that a batch mixes classes, and one object per session violates this~\cite{burns2021bn,gong2022note}. Given mixed batches that a robot would not have, TENT reaches \TtaSupTentIidAcc\pct. For the jitter CNN, every adaptation baseline is below its raw score, and only the state estimate improves it.

The label-free adapter learns from the same decomposition without labels or test data. It lifts frozen Sparsh-I-JEPA to \IjepaAdAcc\pct, against \IjepaAdJitterAcc\pct\ when its positives are made with photometric jitter instead of a state swap, and \IjepaAdNoDecoyAcc\pct\ without the decoys. It is weaker than reading the images relative to the estimate, and it uses the session identity of each training crop.

With stricter thresholds, the 1st and 99th percentiles, it detects both changes later (\FrSweepOneUnit\ and \FrSweepOneGel\ episodes) and raises no false alarm (supplement). A canvas session, whose weave lowers the fine correlation, and the illumination steps between sessions raise no alarm, because they do not occur together. \Cref{tab:detect} applies the same rule to streams spliced from four datasets, with thresholds set on one unit of each and nothing tuned on change points. Our first implementation computed the statistics on the material crops, which cover each frame sparsely and hold one object per window. On the real stream it missed the change of unit, and on spliced streams it detected \TeOursDet\pct\ of the changes. A per-object variant on the crops did no better (supplement).

Detecting the changes matters. A running mean that is never restarted reaches \SrcSupNoResetAcc\pct\ for the CNN. Restarted by the crop-based detector, which missed the change of unit, the estimate for the September sessions still averaged objects of the first unit, and the CNN reached \SrcSupTrkAcc\pct. Restarted by the whole-frame detector it reaches \SrcSupTrkFrAcc\pct, and started afresh at the deployment in September \SrcSupTrkFrDepAcc\pct. The two false alarms cost nothing measurable. A tracker started in September and restarted only at the gel replacement gives \SrcSupTrkDepAcc\pct, because two objects already give a good estimate (\cref{sec:sources}).

\Cref{tab:sources} varies only the test-time estimate. Any unlabelled frames of the deployment work, provided they come from other objects: the previous session, a random other session, all sessions together, or the bare gel. The session's own contacts are the worst contact-based estimate, because their mean contains the object. The number of objects matters more than the number of contacts. An estimate built from one other object gives \CvSupObjOne\pct, from two objects \CvSupObjTwo\pct\ and from nineteen \CvSupObjNineteen\pct. The smooth illumination field does most of the work (\SrcSupLowpassAcc\pct\ alone), a single colour per session recovers part of it, and centring the features does not work. The fine pattern adds little to the correction. The bare gel works as well as the contacts, so standard flat-fielding is a valid estimate of the state where blank frames exist. The contact stream gives the same estimate where they do not, which is the case for archived datasets and for our training campaigns.

\Cref{tab:rotation} holds out each campaign in turn. Time alone is not the problem. Sessions recorded weeks apart on the hardware seen in training are recognised at \FoldBSupRawAcc--\FoldASupRawAcc\pct\ raw (folds A and B), and the state itself changes little over three months. Its fine band still correlates at \HwFarMonths, against \HwFarWeeks\ after weeks, and its illumination field has moved by \LpDistStwoJulSep\ grey levels. Within known states the subtraction is unnecessary and can cost up to \num{10} points, because the training mean $G$ is then a mixture of states that no real session has. The gain in fold D is a gain against a collapse, and the collapse comes from the training set. Trained on A and B alone, with every material recorded on unit 1, the CNN collapses less on D (\TsABSupRawAcc\pct\ raw, \TsABSupRawTop\pct\ in one class), the state estimate adds less (\TsABSupPrevAcc\pct), and the jitter CNN gains nothing from it (\TsABAugRawAcc\ to \TsABAugPrevAcc\pct). Trained on A and C, with four materials again recorded only on unit 2, it collapses again (\TsACSupRawAcc\pct) and recovers to \TsACSupPrevAcc\pct. Trained on June alone, it scores \GapFoneSupBRawAcc\pct\ on the same unit weeks later and \GapFoneSupDRawAcc\pct\ on the other unit, and the estimate does not recover that loss (\GapFoneSupDPrevAcc\pct). The estimate therefore repairs the shortcut that one session per object creates, and it does not repair everything a change of hardware costs.

Two cases are not repaired. In fold C, all five test materials were learned on gel 3 only and are tested on earlier hardware. The four rigid materials score \FoldCSupRawFour\pct\ raw and \FoldCSupPrevFour\pct\ with the state removed, wood scores \FoldCSupRawWood\ and \FoldCSupPrevWood\pct, and frozen Sparsh is at chance. In fold D, the same four materials, now crossing the gel replacement in the other direction, lose accuracy when the collapse is removed. Wood, which crosses both unit and gel in fold D, is recovered there. Both failures involve the gel replacement, but the change of unit also replaced a gel and was corrected, and the four materials are rigid and were recorded in a single session per gel. We cannot separate the gel, the materials and the training-set structure as the cause.

\subsection{Other sensors and the hardware in foundation features}
\label{sec:second}

\paragraph{Foundation features encode the unit.}
Fourteen materials were recorded on both units. A linear probe trained on frozen features of some of these materials, and tested on the others, tells the two units apart from a single crop at \ProbeSdinoRawUnit\pct\ for Sparsh-DINO, \ProbeIjepaRawUnit\ for Sparsh-I-JEPA, \ProbeDinovRawUnit\ for DINOv2 and \ProbeCnnRawUnit\ for our CNN, where chance is 50\pct. Read relative to the previous session's state, the same crops give \ProbeSdinoPrevUnit, \ProbeIjepaPrevUnit, \ProbeDinovPrevUnit\ and \ProbeCnnPrevUnit\pct, while the material probe on the same features barely changes (supplement).

\paragraph{SITR: four GelSight Minis.}
SITR's released classification set has 20 objects pressed on seven sensors, four of them GelSight Minis~\cite{sitr2025}. Following its protocol with a frozen DINOv2, a head trained on one Mini scores \SitrDinovRawIntra\pct\ on the other three raw, \SitrDinovBgIntra\pct\ with SITR's background image subtracted, and \SitrDinovStreamIntra\pct\ relative to the mean of the sensor's own unlabelled images. That mean uses the 16{,}000 images of the sensor's training split, and the test images are not among them. The mean of 100 random images gives \SitrDinovStreamHundredIntra\pct, and the mean of 20 is worse than no correction (\SitrDinovStreamTwentyIntra\pct), because it still contains the objects in those 20 presses. SITR itself reports 90\pct\ on its 16-object version with calibration presses and simulated pretraining, and 73\pct\ for an ImageNet ViT, which matches our raw number.

\paragraph{FEATS: force across units and gels.}
FEATS records indenters with known force maps on five Minis and six gels~\cite{feats2025}. A probe on its no-contact frames identifies the sensor and the gel at \FeatsProbeSensor\pct\ and \FeatsProbeGel\pct, so the state is in the bare frame there as well. A CNN regressing normal force reaches \FeatsRawTestMae\,N on the training hardware and \FeatsRawOldGelMae\,N and \FeatsRawNewGelMae\,N on another unit with its old and a new gel. Relative to the stream estimate the errors are \FeatsStreamOldGelMae\ and \FeatsStreamNewGelMae\,N, and relative to the no-contact frame \FeatsBgOldGelMae\ and \FeatsBgNewGelMae\,N. No image-space reading brings force across hardware close to the training error.

\paragraph{Real Tactile MNIST: a gel swap on another lab's sensor.}
On Real Tactile MNIST (a digit from single touches, 24 runs over three weeks, one gel swap)~\cite{tactilemnist2025}, frozen Sparsh-I-JEPA puts \TmGelIjepaRawTop\pct\ of the touches after the gel swap into one class and recovers from \TmGelIjepaRawAcc\ to \TmGelIjepaPrevAcc\pct\ when read relative to the previous run's state. For the supervised model, per-run AdaBN does better than the state reading there (\TmGelSupAdaRunAcc\ against \TmGelSupPrevAcc\pct\ after the gel swap), because every run contains all ten digits. This is the mixed-class condition that our one-object sessions lack. The label-free adapter did not transfer to coarse digit shape.

\subsection{Normal force}
\label{sec:force}
Force is a matter of mechanics, and the state does not describe it. Whole-frame models trained on June and July to regress normal force from appearance reach $R^2\le\ForceLearnedBestR$ on September and October, even with the state removed. Marker spreading measured against the bare-gel marker grid does transfer. A linear map of three affine statistics of the displacement field, fitted on June and July, predicts September and October at $r=\ForceGeomXeraC$ ($R^2=\ForceGeomXeraR$). Contact behaves in the same way. A detector that compares the embedding with the session's own bare gel rejects \ContactRefSpec\pct\ of other sessions' bare-gel frames, against \ContactRawSpec\pct\ without that reference (supplement).
