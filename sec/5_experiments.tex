\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\paragraph{Protocol.}
We test every session in which a material is touched with a different unit or gel than in training. Three folds hold out campaign D, campaign C, and campaigns A and B together, and each trains on the remaining campaigns (\cref{tab:folds}). This gives 55 test sessions. In 47 of them both the unit and the gel differ from training, and in 8 only the gel does. Every choice of the method, including the low-pass width and the detector's constants, was made on the fold that holds out D. For the fold that holds out A and B, which trains on later campaigns and tests on earlier ones, we committed a dated pre-registration with three directional predictions before training any model, and all three held (\supp{G}). We report the share of a session's episodes classified correctly, averaged over the 55 sessions, with 95\pct\ bootstrap intervals over sessions, paired for differences between methods. We also report the share of test episodes assigned to the most predicted class, averaged over the folds, which exposes a collapse onto few classes.

\paragraph{Training and the stream.}
Unless stated otherwise, every method uses an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50~\cite{he2016resnet}, fine-tuned at the native \SI{11.3}{\micro\metre}/px on 192-px crops along the contact, which we call tube crops, with flips, quarter turns and photometric jitter, for 6{,}000 AdamW steps at batch size 64 (\supp{A}). We call this source-only training. An episode is classified by the mean log-probability of 24 crops, from three positions along the contact and eight frames of the stroke. Table rows average three training seeds, or five for the closest comparisons. Every adaptation method receives the same unlabeled stream: all contacts recorded on the test hardware in the test campaign. The stream therefore contains the test sessions, as a working robot's stream would, and row~i of \cref{tab:ablation} scores sessions it never contained. All methods are given the true change dates, so the correction and the test-time baselines reset together (\cref{sec:trackerexp} uses the detector's alarms).

\subsection{Comparison with published methods}
\label{sec:baselines}

Every published method in \cref{tab:baselines} runs with its paper's default settings on the same encoder, training budget and stream. SDAT and MIC keep the better of two trade-off weights, chosen on the first seed, which favors them over our fixed weight (\supp{D}). Source-only, the ResNet-50 scores \XpRecipeAcc\pct, and \XpRecipeTop\pct\ of its test episodes go to one class.

\paragraph{Test time.}
Four of the five test-time methods collapse on the robot's stream, to about \XpTtaBnAcc\pct. Per-batch BatchNorm, Tent, SAR and DeYO estimate normalization statistics from the current batch, here 72 crops from three consecutive episodes of one session, so every batch holds a single material and the statistics normalize it away. RoTTA, designed for correlated streams, is the one test-time method that works on this stream, gaining \XdRottaVsSrc\ points over the unadapted model. Image correction performs comparably without batch statistics or gradient updates. It raises the unadapted model from \XpSrcAcc\ to \XpSrcReadAcc\pct, \XdSrcReadVsRotta\ points above RoTTA with an interval that includes zero (\XdSrcReadVsRottaLo\ to \XdSrcReadVsRottaHi). The two methods differ on the eight sessions of the other gel, where RoTTA falls from \XpSrcGel\ to \XpTtaRottaGel\pct\ and the correction only to \XpSrcReadGel\pct.

\paragraph{Retraining.}
Every retraining method improves on source-only training, and the correction improves each of them further on the 55 sessions, by three to ten points with intervals that exclude zero. The gain comes from the 47 sessions of the other unit and gel. On the eight sessions of the other gel the correction lowers every method (\cref{sec:discussion}). With the correction, DANN, SDAT, MIC and CDAN+E end within three points of each other, while MCC stays below. Our method, CDAN+E with the correction, is the highest at \XpFullAcc\pct, but its lead is within the interval: \XdFullVsDannRead\ points above corrected DANN over five seeds (\XdFullVsDannReadLo\ to \XdFullVsDannReadHi), \XdFullVsSdatRead\ above SDAT and \XdFullVsMicRead\ above MIC. The differences between aligners are smaller than the gain from adding the correction. FDA~\cite{fda2020}, which transfers the stream's low-frequency spectrum to the training images, gains half as much as the correction alone (\supp{D}).

\subsection{What each part contributes}
\label{sec:ablation}

\Cref{tab:ablation} adds the parts one at a time. The correction alone gains about twelve points without any training (b). Alignment alone gains more (c), and combining them is better than either alone (d). Subtracting the whole estimate from the full method's test images, fine pattern included, lowers its accuracy by about forty points, far below source-only training, as \cref{eq:residue} predicts. Narrower low-passes ($\sigma$ of 8 or 16\,px) lose ten to twenty points, and wider ones (66 or 132\,px) lose up to two. The width was set on the fold that holds out D, whose 20 sessions are part of this sweep. With DANN in place of CDAN+E the method loses less than a point (e). Every part helps on the two folds that cross units. On the fold that holds out C, which contains four other-gel sessions and one wood session, none does.

A confidence objective that spreads predictions over the classes~\cite{zhang2022memo,liang2020shot} does not help, whether applied to the average of several contacts (f) or to every contact (g, \supp{D}). Correcting the training images too costs about two points (h).

Row d is transductive, because the stream already contains the test contacts. To separate adaptation from memorization, we align with the first half of each configuration's test sessions and score the second half (i). About three quarters of the alignment gain remains, so most of it is adaptation to the hardware rather than to the test contacts themselves.

\subsection{Other encoders and views}
\label{sec:encoders}

\Cref{tab:encoders} adds other encoders and a small CNN trained from scratch. On tube crops every encoder collapses when trained source-only, the correction raises each by ten to twenty points, and alignment adds to the correction for every encoder. On 384-px crops placed anywhere in the frame, which contain much of the illumination field, alignment alone improves every encoder substantially, and the correction adds \XdDinoFrameReadVsCdan\ points for DINOv2-B but lowers ResNet-50 by nine (\supp{X}). This frame view was evaluated after the pre-registered tube view.

\subsection{Sensors with broader appearance differences}
\label{sec:second}

SITR's Minis see different parts of their gels and differ in contrast by a factor of \SmContrastRatio\ (\supp{K}). Subtracting the mean image of the test Mini, in the full band, lets a frozen DINOv2 with an MLP head recover from \CfSitrRawOneCross\ to \CfSitrStreamOneCross\pct\ on the classes trained on the other Mini when one class is tied to the test Mini (\cref{fig:confound}). It leaves a CNN trained from scratch at about \SmStreamCross\pct, because that CNN also learns the field of view and the contrast, which no per-pixel correction removes. With four classes tied to the test Mini and its unlabeled images as the stream, CDAN+E raises such a CNN from \XsJit\ to \XsCdan\pct\ on the other 16 classes (\supp{L}). With only 5 of the 20 classes in the stream the gain shrinks by about half, so the stream must cover the robot's materials.

\subsection{The estimate in practice}
\label{sec:practice}
\label{sec:trackerexp}

For the CNN trained from scratch on campaign D, an estimate built from the contacts of one other object gives \CvsSupObjOne\pct, two objects give \CvsSupObjTwo\pct, and all nineteen others give \CvsSupObjNineteen\pct\ (\supp{Q}). The smooth component therefore converges after about two objects, as \cref{eq:residue} suggests. Run over all \EpRecorded\ episodes in recording order, the detector of \cref{sec:estimate} raises \FrAlarms\ alarms: \FrUnitLatency\ episodes after the change of unit, \FrGelLatency\ after the gel replacement, and \FrFalseAlarms\ false ones (\supp{U}). Restarting the estimate at its alarms instead of the true dates changes the full method's accuracy by \XdFullVarDetVsLp\ points (\XdFullVarDetVsLpLo\ to \XdFullVarDetVsLpHi).
