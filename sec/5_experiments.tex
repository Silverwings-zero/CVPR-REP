\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\paragraph{Protocol.}
Material: each campaign is held out in turn (\cref{sec:timeorstate}); the fold that trains on A+B+C (June--July, 1{,}360 episodes) and tests on D (September--October, 1{,}351 episodes, 20 materials, chance 5\pct) is the deployment case and carries the main tables: the second unit appears in its training data for four rigid materials only (July 10--13), so fifteen materials are tested on a unit they were never recorded on (S1$\rightarrow$S2) and five on a replaced gel of the unit they were recorded on (S2$\rightarrow$S3); inputs are single native 192-px crops (11.3\,\si{\micro\metre}/px; 12 per episode; frame integration and resolution are ablated in \cref{sec:ablation}); per episode, logits of its crops are pooled; we report macro accuracy, macro-F1 (collapse onto a few classes leaves macro accuracy unchanged and F1 not) and the share of test episodes assigned to the most predicted class. Heads are MLPs on frozen embeddings, three seeds; labels are used only in the heads. When test embeddings are state-calibrated, the training embeddings are calibrated with \emph{their} previous session's state as well, so that the head sees one transform at both ends; raw rows use raw embeddings at both ends. Because campaign D records every material in exactly one session, materials are the unit of statistics: we give paired bootstrap intervals over the 20 materials for the claims we make. The era probe (given the material, which campaign) measures campaign-identifiability of a representation on the training campaigns (chance \num{57.7}\pct, lower is better). Every design choice was selected on campaign D; the other three folds of \cref{sec:timeorstate} are independent of that.

\paragraph{The tracker on the stream.}
Run over all \TrkEpisodes\ episodes of the four months in order (\cref{fig:tracker}), Algorithm~\ref{alg:tracker} with thresholds set on the first hardware state (\TrkSettingsGood\ of \TrkSettingsTotal\ settings do so) raises exactly two alarms: \TrkUnitLatency\ episodes after the change of unit and \TrkGelLatency\ after the gel replacement, and none in the other \TrkSessions\ sessions, including a canvas session whose own weave hides the pattern and a power-cycle jump of the illumination within a hardware state. Reading campaign D relative to the tracker's own estimate, with its automatic restarts, gives \TrkSupTrkAcc\pct\ for the plain CNN (\TrkSupTrkCrossUnit\pct\ on the cross-unit materials), against \TrkSupNoResetAcc\pct\ for a running mean that is never restarted and \TrkSupOracleAcc\pct\ for the previous session chosen by hand. On Real Tactile MNIST the same rule, calibrated on the first gel's first days, detects the gel swap within \TmTrkLatency\ touches and raises no other alarm in \TmTrkTouches\ touches over \TmTrkRuns\ runs.

\paragraph{Which estimate of the state.}
\Cref{tab:sources} varies only the test-time estimate $\hat F$ for the trained single-frame CNNs. Any unlabelled frames of the current deployment serve: the previous session, a random other session of the test campaign, the mean field of all its sessions (twenty different objects whose imprints average out), or the bare gel, all land within \SrcSupRandAcc--\SrcSupBlankAcc\pct\ for the plain CNN and \SrcAugRandAcc--\SrcAugPrevAcc\pct\ with jitter, against \SrcSupNoneAcc\ and \SrcAugNoneAcc\ raw. The session's own contacts are the worst contact-based estimate (\SrcSupOwnAcc), because they contain the object. That the bare gel works (\SrcSupBlankAcc) means standard flat-fielding is a valid estimate of the state where blank frames exist; the contact stream gives an equivalent one where they do not, which is the case for every archive and for our training campaigns. The spatially varying low-frequency field does most of the work (\SrcSupLowpassAcc); a single colour per session recovers part of it (\SrcSupMeancolAcc); re-centring in feature space does not (\SrcSupFeatAcc). The fine-scale part of the field adds a few points for the jitter model and none for the plain one.

\paragraph{Photometric jitter.} Standard photometric augmentation (per-channel gain $\pm$20\pct\ and a smooth multiplicative field $\pm$15\pct) is, with single-frame inputs, a strong baseline on its own: \af{\SupAugFoneRawSAcc}{\SupAugFoneRawSF}\ raw (three seeds, $\pm$\SupAugFoneRawSAccSd) and \af{\SupAugFonePrevSAcc}{\SupAugFonePrevSF}\ with the previous session's state---the best deployable pipeline when labels are available. It is a regulariser against the state rather than a better representation: on the same-state folds of \cref{sec:timeorstate} it adds \num{-3} to \num{+7} points instead of \num{28}; on a balanced training set it absorbs the state change on its own (\cref{sec:timeorstate}); and on eight-frame inputs, where the fine-scale state is easier to learn, it gives only \SupAugRawSAcc\ raw (\cref{sec:ablation}).

\paragraph{Without labels.} The label-free state-swap adapter needs neither labels nor test-time data: it lifts frozen Sparsh-I-JEPA to \af{\IjepaAdAcc}{\IjepaAdF}\ (three seeds, $\pm$\IjepaAdAccSd; $+$\num{24.0} $[+13.9, +35.9]$), Sparsh-MAE to \MaeAdAcc\ and Sparsh-DINO to \SdinoAdAcc, with the era probe falling from \num{77}--\num{79} to \num{66}--\num{69}. The state swap is what gives the gain: the same adapter trained with photometric-jitter positives instead reaches \IjepaAdJitterAcc\pct. On the general-vision DINOv2~\cite{oquab2024dinov2} the adapter adds only \num{3.8} $[-4.3,+11.5]$: the objective reorganises what tactile pretraining already holds. Trained from scratch, the objective is a weak representation on its own (\af{\SwapFoneRawSAcc}{\SwapFoneRawSF}, three seeds; \HoldSwapRawAcc\pct\ even within a campaign): its value is on top of a pretrained encoder. It is the best option without labels; with labels, jitter and calibration do better.

\paragraph{Adaptation baselines.}
AdaBN~\cite{li2018adabn} re-estimated over the whole test campaign reaches \SupFoneAdaAcc\pct\ for the single-frame supervised CNN, close to calibration on this split---but it pools 20 objects into one statistic, and on the other folds it moves by \num{-25} to \num{+4} (\cref{sec:timeorstate}). Re-estimated per session, which is what a robot has, it gives \SupFoneAdaSessAcc\pct\ for the supervised CNN and 3--9\pct\ for every other model: with one object per session the statistics absorb the object. A session-adversarial head (DANN~\cite{ganin2016dann}, gradient reversal on the session identity) changes nothing (\cref{tab:material}): session identity is not what the encoder uses, the state is.

\paragraph{With a few target labels.}
Calibration stacks with labels. Adding 1\pct\ of the test campaign's episodes to the head's training set gives \SupFoneRawFSOne\pct\ raw and \SupFonePrevFSOne\pct\ calibrated; 5\pct: \SupFoneRawFSFive\ and \SupFonePrevFSFive; 10\pct: \SupFoneRawFSTen\ and \SupFonePrevFSTen. Two of the twenty materials were replaced by new pieces of the same kind before campaign D (textured rubber, EPDM foam); excluding them, the raw jitter CNN scores \SupAugFoneRawEighteen\pct\ (18-way).

\Cref{tab:rotation} holds out each campaign in turn. In folds A and B every test material has a session on the same unit and gel in the training data (A and B are both S1). In fold C, four of the five test materials were learned on gel 3 and are tested on gel 2 of the same unit, with gel 2 in the training data for the other fifteen classes; the fifth (wood) was learned on unit 2 and is tested on unit 1. Fold D is the deployment case above. Three things follow.

\emph{Time is not the cause.} Sessions the model never saw, recorded weeks apart on the same hardware, are recognised at \FoldASupRawAcc--\FoldBAugRawAcc\pct\ raw (A, B); the state itself is unchanged after three months (\HwMonths\ in the fine band, \LpDistStwoJulSep\ grey levels in the illumination field). The failure is the change of hardware, and it has a target: the three-campaign model learned the second unit on four rigid materials, so under that unit's state everything looks like them (one of them absorbs \SupFoneRawSTop\pct\ of the later campaign). Subtracting the state undoes exactly that. No material was recorded on the same hardware months apart, so the months-later claim is made for the state, not for a classifier.

\emph{State subtraction pays where the test classes were never seen in the test state.} In D it undoes the cross-unit collapse (\DOneSupRaw$\rightarrow$\DOneSupPrev--\DOneSupBlank\ on the fifteen materials). In A and B, where every test class was seen on the test hardware, every estimate is neutral to harmful (\num{-10} to \num{+3}): the training-mean state is then a mixture no real session has. Across the gel replacement---the five rigid materials in fold D (\DTwoSupRaw$\rightarrow$\DTwoSupPrev) and the four in fold C (\FoldCSupRawAcc$\rightarrow$\FoldCSupPrevAcc)---nothing in the state family helps and frozen Sparsh is at chance. The force result says a new gel changes mechanics; the materials concerned are also the five rigid ones, whose fine texture is weakest (own-session pattern 0.17), and the data cannot separate the two. The method is a fix for a rendering state that the test classes were never imaged in, and nothing more.

\emph{The subtraction belongs at test time.} Training the encoder on state-subtracted inputs is worse than raw training with test-time subtraction in every fold (\FoldAAutoPrevAcc\ vs \FoldASupRawAcc, \FoldBAutoPrevAcc\ vs \FoldBSupRawAcc, \FoldCAutoPrevAcc\ vs \FoldCSupRawAcc, \FoldDAutoOwnAcc\ vs \SrcSupBlankAcc): the encoder is better off seeing real states. Photometric jitter is the most robust single choice---best, or within three points of the best raw model, in every fold, and the only thing that helps across the gel. Whole-campaign AdaBN moves by \num{-25} to \num{+4} across folds; per session it collapses in every one. Eight-frame integration hurts in the same-state case too (train A+C, test B: \HoldFeightRawAcc\ vs \HoldSupRawAcc\ for single frames): what it exposes is the session-level part of the state (own session \SigCOwn\ vs other sessions of the campaign \SigSameEraOtherDay\ in \cref{fig:signature}), which every new session changes.

\paragraph{Training-set structure.}
Removing the second unit's July sessions from training removes the collapse. Trained on A+B (unit 1 only, every material in two sessions) the plain CNN scores \TsABSupRawAcc\pct\ raw on D, \TsABSupPrevAcc\ with the previous session's state and \TsABSupOwnAcc\ with the session's own contacts; with jitter \TsABAugRawAcc\ raw and \TsABAugPrevAcc\ calibrated. Trained on A+C (one session per material, unit 2 present for four classes) it collapses again: \TsACSupRawAcc\ raw, \TsACSupPrevAcc\ calibrated. On the 13 materials common to every split (the A/B materials minus the two replaced pieces), the change of unit costs the plain CNN 15 points (\ThirteenAtoBRaw\ on the same hardware $\rightarrow$ \ThirteenABtoDRaw), of which the state estimate recovers 9--17 (\ThirteenABtoDPrev--\ThirteenABtoDOwn); the jitter CNN keeps \ThirteenABtoDAugRaw\ raw, the same-hardware raw level, and gains nothing from calibration; the three-campaign CNN falls to \ThirteenABCtoDRaw\ and recovers to \ThirteenABCtoDPrev--\ThirteenABCtoDBlank. State subtraction is thus the fix for a collapse that any training set invites in which some classes identify a hardware state---and one session per object is how tactile datasets are recorded; for a training set on one unit, photometric jitter alone absorbs the change of unit and the estimate adds 9--17 points to a plain CNN.

\subsection{Normal force: geometry survives, appearance does not}
\label{sec:force}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig/fig_force.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{Force across hardware needs geometry against the unloaded gel.} $R^2$ of log normal force on the later campaign.}
  \label{fig:force}

Normal force cannot be read from appearance across a change of hardware: whole-frame representations trained on native frames---label-free, supervised, and with the force signal in the objective---reach $R^2\le\ForceLearnedBestR$ on the later campaign, and model-free deformation statistics against a fresh bare-gel frame reach \num{0.22}. Marker spreading against the bare-gel marker grid does transfer (\cref{fig:force}): within a session the affine divergence tracks the Nano17 force at Spearman \ForceWithinRho; a linear map of three affine statistics fitted on the first 13 sessions of campaign D predicts the last 7 at $R^2$ \ForceGeomFreshR\ ($r$ \ForceGeomFreshC) with a fresh grid and \ForceGeomPrevR\ ($r$ \ForceGeomPrevC) with the previous session's grid; fitted on June--July with a single bare-gel grid borrowed from September, it predicts September--October at $R^2$ \ForceGeomXeraR\ ($r$ \ForceGeomXeraC). A new gel keeps the force ranking ($r\approx0.70$) but changes the force-to-displacement scale: a gel replacement changes mechanics as well as rendering, and absolute force needs one scale calibration per gel.

\subsection{Contact}
\label{sec:contact}
Contact detectors trained on frozen whole-frame features on the seven lead-in sessions detect contact in the other sessions at $\ge$98\pct\ recall but reject \ContactRawSpec\pct\ of those sessions' bare-gel frames: another session's unloaded gel looks like contact. Referencing the embedding to the session's own bare gel restores \ContactRefSpec\pct\ specificity at \ContactRefRecall\pct\ recall (differencing the raw frame against the blank: \ContactBlankdiffSpec\pct). The unloaded state is the reference for contact as for force.

\subsection{Second unit}
\label{sec:second}
On Real Tactile MNIST (digit from single touches, 10-way, two events: later days on the same gel; the gel swap), every encoder again collapses raw (supervised \TmLaterSupRawTop\pct\ of predictions in one class, frozen Sparsh \TmLaterIjepaRawTop\pct) and recovers with the previous run's state (supervised F1 \TmLaterSupRawF$\rightarrow$\TmLaterSupPrevF; frozen Sparsh \TmLaterIjepaRawAcc$\rightarrow$\TmLaterIjepaPrevAcc\pct; across the gel swap \TmGelIjepaRawAcc$\rightarrow$\TmGelIjepaPrevAcc). Per-run AdaBN works there (\TmLaterSupAdaRunAcc\pct) because every run holds all ten digits---the control for the per-session collapse on our data. The label-free swap did not transfer to coarse digit shape on this dataset; we claim it for texture on our unit.

\subsection{Ablations}
\label{sec:ablation}
\paragraph{Frames integrated.} The imprint is static during a stroke, so consecutive native frames can be averaged; this raises the reliability of fine texture but lowers accuracy on new sessions. With the supervised CNN and previous-session calibration: one frame \SupFonePrevSAcc\pct\ $\rightarrow$ two \SupFtwoPrevAcc\ $\rightarrow$ four \SupFfourPrevAcc\ $\rightarrow$ eight \SupPrevAcc; with jitter, raw \SupAugFoneRawSAcc\ (one) vs \SupAugRawSAcc\ (eight); within the campaign, \HoldSupRawAcc\ (one) vs \HoldFeightRawAcc\ (eight). The label-free objective is insensitive to it (\SwapFoneRawSAcc\ vs \SwapRawAcc). Adding single-frame-level pixel noise to the eight-frame means at training and test time does not recover the single-frame result (\NoiseFeightPrevAcc\ vs \SupPrevAcc\ with eight frames and \SupFonePrevSAcc\ with one): the advantage is not a noise-regularisation effect, and we report it as a finding.
\paragraph{Resolution.} Area-downscaling the native crops twofold does not reduce cross-hardware material accuracy (single frame, calibrated: \SupFoneScHalfPrevSAcc\ vs \SupFonePrevRawHeadAcc\ native under the same heads; eight frames: \SupScHalfOwnAcc\ vs \SupOwnAcc); fourfold costs \num{1.5}--\num{3} points (\SupFoneScQuarterPrevSAcc; \SupScQuarterOwnAcc). The material cue that survives the state change lives above $\sim$45\,\si{\micro\metre}; the finest band is unreliable in one frame (0.14) and mostly state when integrated.
\paragraph{Decoys.} Removing the position-matched decoy negatives from the adapter objective costs \num{3.1} points $[+0.5,+6.1]$ (\IjepaAdNoDecoyAcc\ vs \IjepaAdAcc).
