\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\paragraph{Protocol.}
Material: train on campaigns A+B+C (June--July, 1{,}360 episodes), test on D (September--October, 1{,}351 episodes, 20 materials, chance 5\pct); inputs are single native 192-px crops (11.3\,\si{\micro\metre}/px; 12 per episode; frame integration and resolution are ablated in \cref{sec:ablation}); per episode, logits of its crops are pooled; we report macro accuracy, macro-F1 (collapse onto a few classes leaves macro accuracy unchanged and F1 not) and the share of test episodes assigned to the most predicted class. Heads are MLPs on frozen embeddings, three seeds; labels are used only in the heads. When test embeddings are state-calibrated, the training embeddings are calibrated with \emph{their} previous session's state as well, so that the head sees one transform at both ends; raw rows use raw embeddings at both ends. Because campaign D records every material in exactly one session, materials are the unit of statistics: we give paired bootstrap intervals over the 20 materials for the claims we make. The era probe (given the material, which campaign) measures campaign-identifiability of a representation on the training campaigns (chance \num{57.7}\pct, lower is better). Every design choice was selected on campaign D; \cref{sec:timeorstate} checks the ones that matter on an independent split.

\paragraph{Which estimate of the state.}
\Cref{tab:sources} varies only the test-time estimate $\hat F$ for the trained single-frame CNNs. Any contacts of the current deployment serve: a random other session of the test campaign works as well as the previous one, and the mean field of all its sessions---twenty different objects whose imprints average out---is the best estimate (\SrcSupCampAcc\pct). The session's own contacts are the worst contact-based estimate (\SrcSupOwnAcc), because they contain the object. The bare gel works too (\SrcSupBlankAcc): standard flat-fielding is a valid estimate of the state where blank frames exist; the contact stream gives an equivalent one where they do not, which is the case for every archive and for our training campaigns. The spatially varying low-frequency field does most of the work (\SrcSupLowpassAcc); a single colour per session (\SrcSupMeancolAcc) or re-centring in feature space (\SrcSupFeatAcc) do not. The fine-scale part of the field adds the rest.

\paragraph{Photometric jitter.} Standard photometric augmentation (per-channel gain $\pm$20\pct\ and a smooth multiplicative field $\pm$15\pct) is, with single-frame inputs, a strong baseline on its own: \af{\SupAugFoneRawSAcc}{\SupAugFoneRawSF}\ raw (three seeds, $\pm$\SupAugFoneRawSAccSd) and \af{\SupAugFonePrevSAcc}{\SupAugFonePrevSF}\ with the previous session's state---the best deployable pipeline when labels are available. It is a regulariser against the state rather than a better representation: on the within-campaign split of \cref{sec:timeorstate}, where the state does not change, it adds \num{3} points instead of \num{28}, and on eight-frame inputs, where the fine-scale state is easier to learn, it gives only \SupAugRawSAcc\ raw (\cref{sec:ablation}).

\paragraph{Without labels.} The label-free state-swap adapter needs neither labels nor test-time data: it lifts frozen Sparsh-I-JEPA to \af{\IjepaAdAcc}{\IjepaAdF}\ (three seeds, $\pm$\IjepaAdAccSd; $+$\num{24.0} $[+13.9, +35.9]$), Sparsh-MAE to \MaeAdAcc\ and Sparsh-DINO to \SdinoAdAcc, with the era probe falling from \num{77}--\num{79} to \num{66}--\num{69}. The state swap is what gives the gain: the same adapter trained with photometric-jitter positives instead reaches \IjepaAdJitterAcc\pct. On the general-vision DINOv2~\cite{oquab2024dinov2} the adapter adds only \num{3.8} $[-4.3,+11.5]$: the objective reorganises what tactile pretraining already holds. Trained from scratch, the objective is a weak representation on its own (\af{\SwapFoneRawSAcc}{\SwapFoneRawSF}, three seeds; \HoldSwapRawAcc\pct\ even within a campaign): its value is on top of a pretrained encoder. It is the best option without labels; with labels, jitter and calibration do better.

\paragraph{Adaptation baselines.}
AdaBN~\cite{li2018adabn} re-estimated over the whole test campaign reaches \SupFoneAdaAcc\pct\ for the single-frame supervised CNN, close to calibration on this split---but it pools 20 objects into one statistic, and \cref{sec:timeorstate} shows that it is not safe. Re-estimated per session, which is what a robot has, it gives \SupFoneAdaSessAcc\pct\ for the supervised CNN and 3--9\pct\ for every other model: with one object per session the statistics absorb the object. A session-adversarial head (DANN~\cite{ganin2016dann}, gradient reversal on the session identity) changes nothing (\cref{tab:material}): session identity is not what the encoder uses, the state is.

\paragraph{With a few target labels.}
Calibration stacks with labels. Adding 1\pct\ of the test campaign's episodes to the head's training set gives \SupFoneRawFSOne\pct\ raw and \SupFonePrevFSOne\pct\ calibrated; 5\pct: \SupFoneRawFSFive\ and \SupFonePrevFSFive; 10\pct: \SupFoneRawFSTen\ and \SupFonePrevFSTen. Two of the twenty materials were replaced by new pieces of the same kind before campaign D (textured rubber, EPDM foam); excluding them, the raw jitter CNN scores \SupAugFoneRawEighteen\pct\ (18-way).

If the failure were a matter of elapsed time, a model should degrade on any later session. It does not. Trained on campaigns A and C and tested on B---sessions it never saw, recorded weeks after A, under the same rig state---the raw supervised CNN scores \HoldSupRawAcc\pct\ (\cref{tab:holdout}), against \SupFoneRawSAcc\pct\ across the campaign break. The same test separates the methods. Calibration is safe: it adds \num{5} points where the state has not changed and \num{34} where it has, and reaches most of the within-campaign level (\SupFonePrevSAcc--\SrcSupCampAcc\ vs \HoldSupPrevAcc\pct). AdaBN is not: re-estimated over a campaign whose state did not change it \emph{costs} \num{7}--\num{16} points, and per session it collapses on both splits. Jitter adds \num{3} points here and \num{28} across the break. Eight-frame integration hurts even within the campaign (\HoldFeightRawAcc\ vs \HoldSupRawAcc): what it exposes is the session-level part of the state (own session \SigCOwn\ vs other sessions of the campaign \SigSameEraOtherDay\ in \cref{fig:signature}), which every new session changes.

\subsection{Normal force: geometry survives, appearance does not}
\label{sec:force}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig/fig_force.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{Force across campaigns needs geometry against the unloaded gel.} $R^2$ of log normal force on the later campaign.}
  \label{fig:force}
\end{figure}

Normal force cannot be read from appearance across campaigns: whole-frame representations trained on native frames---label-free, supervised, and with the force signal in the objective---reach $R^2\le\ForceLearnedBestR$ on the later campaign, and model-free deformation statistics against a fresh bare-gel frame reach \num{0.22}. Marker spreading against the bare-gel marker grid does transfer (\cref{fig:force}): within a session the affine divergence tracks the Nano17 force at Spearman \ForceWithinRho; a linear map of three affine statistics fitted on the first 13 sessions of campaign D predicts the last 7 at $R^2$ \ForceGeomFreshR\ ($r$ \ForceGeomFreshC) with a fresh grid and \ForceGeomPrevR\ ($r$ \ForceGeomPrevC) with the previous session's grid; fitted on June--July with a single bare-gel grid borrowed from September, it predicts September--October at $R^2$ \ForceGeomXeraR\ ($r$ \ForceGeomXeraC). A new gel keeps the force ranking ($r\approx0.70$) but changes the force-to-displacement scale: a gel replacement changes mechanics as well as rendering, and absolute force needs one scale calibration per gel.

\subsection{Contact}
\label{sec:contact}
Contact detectors trained on frozen whole-frame features on the seven lead-in sessions detect contact in the other sessions at $\ge$98\pct\ recall but reject \ContactRawSpec\pct\ of those sessions' bare-gel frames: another session's unloaded gel looks like contact. Referencing the embedding to the session's own bare gel restores \ContactRefSpec\pct\ specificity at \ContactRefRecall\pct\ recall (differencing the raw frame against the blank: \ContactBlankdiffSpec\pct). The unloaded state is the reference for contact as for force.

\subsection{Second unit}
\label{sec:second}
On Real Tactile MNIST (digit from single touches, 10-way, two events: later days on the same gel; the gel swap), every encoder again collapses raw (supervised \TmLaterSupRawTop\pct\ of predictions in one class, frozen Sparsh \TmLaterIjepaRawTop\pct) and recovers with the previous run's state (supervised F1 \TmLaterSupRawF$\rightarrow$\TmLaterSupPrevF; frozen Sparsh \TmLaterIjepaRawAcc$\rightarrow$\TmLaterIjepaPrevAcc\pct; across the gel swap \TmGelIjepaRawAcc$\rightarrow$\TmGelIjepaPrevAcc). Per-run AdaBN works there (\TmLaterSupAdaRunAcc\pct) because every run holds all ten digits---the control for the per-session collapse on our data. The label-free swap did not transfer to coarse digit shape on this dataset; we claim it for texture on our unit.

\subsection{Ablations}
\label{sec:ablation}
\paragraph{Frames integrated.} The imprint is static during a stroke, so consecutive native frames can be averaged; this raises the reliability of fine texture but lowers accuracy on new sessions. With the supervised CNN and previous-session calibration: one frame \SupFonePrevSAcc\pct\ $\rightarrow$ two \SupFtwoPrevAcc\ $\rightarrow$ four \SupFfourPrevAcc\ $\rightarrow$ eight \SupPrevAcc; with jitter, raw \SupAugFoneRawSAcc\ (one) vs \SupAugRawSAcc\ (eight); within the campaign, \HoldSupRawAcc\ (one) vs \HoldFeightRawAcc\ (eight). The label-free objective is insensitive to it (\SwapFoneRawSAcc\ vs \SwapRawAcc). Adding single-frame-level pixel noise to the eight-frame means at training and test time does not recover the single-frame result (\NoiseFeightPrevAcc\ vs \SupPrevAcc\ with eight frames and \SupFonePrevSAcc\ with one): the advantage is not a noise-regularisation effect, and we report it as a finding.
\paragraph{Resolution.} Area-downscaling the native crops twofold does not reduce cross-campaign material accuracy (single frame, calibrated: \SupFoneScHalfPrevSAcc\ vs \SupFonePrevRawHeadAcc\ native under the same heads; eight frames: \SupScHalfOwnAcc\ vs \SupOwnAcc); fourfold costs \num{1.5}--\num{3} points (\SupFoneScQuarterPrevSAcc; \SupScQuarterOwnAcc). The material cue that survives the state change lives above $\sim$45\,\si{\micro\metre}; the finest band is unreliable in one frame (0.14) and mostly state when integrated.
\paragraph{Decoys.} Removing the position-matched decoy negatives from the adapter objective costs \num{3.1} points $[+0.5,+6.1]$ (\IjepaAdNoDecoyAcc\ vs \IjepaAdAcc).
