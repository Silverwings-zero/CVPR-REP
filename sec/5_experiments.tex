\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\paragraph{Protocol.}
We test every recording of a material made on hardware other than the one it was learned on. Three folds hold out campaign D, campaign C, and campaigns A and B together, and each trains on the remaining campaigns (\cref{tab:folds}). This gives 55 test sessions. In 47 of them the material was learned on the other unit and gel, and in 8 on the other gel of the same unit. Every choice of the method, including the low-pass width and the detector's constants, was made on the fold that holds out D. For the fold that holds out A and B, which trains on later campaigns and tests on earlier ones, we committed a dated pre-registration with three directional predictions before training any model, and all three held (supplement). We report the share of a session's episodes classified correctly, averaged over the 55 sessions, with 95\pct\ bootstrap intervals over sessions, paired for differences between methods. We also report the share of test episodes assigned to the most predicted class, averaged over the folds, which exposes a collapse onto few classes.

\paragraph{Training and the stream.}
Unless stated otherwise, every method uses an ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-50~\cite{he2016resnet}, fine-tuned at the native \SI{11.3}{\micro\metre}/px on 192-px crops along the contact, which we call tube crops, with flips, quarter turns and photometric jitter, for 6{,}000 AdamW steps at batch size 64 (supplement). We call this training the recipe. An episode is classified by the mean log-probability of 24 crops, from three positions along the contact and eight frames of the stroke. Table rows average three training seeds, or five for the closest comparisons. Over seeds, the 55-session mean varies by under a point for the recipe and the correction and by one to three points for the aligned models, less than the intervals over sessions. Every adaptation method receives the same unlabeled stream: all contacts recorded on the test hardware in the test campaign. The stream therefore contains the test sessions, as a working robot's stream would, and row~i of \cref{tab:ablation} scores sessions it never contained. All methods are given the true change dates, so the correction and the test-time baselines reset together (\cref{sec:trackerexp} uses the detector's alarms).

\subsection{Comparison with published methods}
\label{sec:baselines}

Every published method in \cref{tab:baselines} runs with its paper's default settings on the same encoder, training budget and stream. SDAT and MIC keep the better of two trade-off weights, judged on the first seed of this protocol, which favors them over our method, whose weight is fixed (supplement). Trained with the recipe, the ResNet-50 recognizes the materials of the 55 sessions at \XpRecipeAcc\pct, and on average over the folds \XpRecipeTop\pct\ of the test episodes go to a single class.

\paragraph{Test time.}
Four of the five test-time methods collapse on the robot's stream, to about \XpTtaBnAcc\pct. Per-batch BatchNorm, Tent, SAR and DeYO estimate normalization statistics from the current batch, here 72 crops from three consecutive episodes of one session, so every batch holds a single material and the statistics normalize it away. With the episodes of each configuration shuffled, as in their own benchmarks, they recover to \XpTtaBnIidAcc\pct, just above the unadapted model. RoTTA, designed for correlated streams, is the one test-time method that works on this stream, gaining \XdRottaVsSrc\ points over the unadapted model. Image correction needs neither batches nor gradient steps, and does as well. It raises the unadapted model from \XpSrcAcc\ to \XpSrcReadAcc\pct, \XdSrcReadVsRotta\ points above RoTTA with an interval that includes zero (\XdSrcReadVsRottaLo\ to \XdSrcReadVsRottaHi). The two gains differ: on the eight sessions of the other gel RoTTA falls from \XpSrcGel\ to \XpTtaRottaGel\pct\ and the correction only to \XpSrcReadGel\pct.

\paragraph{Retraining.}
Every retraining method improves on the recipe, and the correction improves each of them further on the 55 sessions, by three to ten points with intervals that exclude zero. The gain comes from the 47 sessions of the other unit and gel. On the eight sessions of the other gel the correction lowers every method (\cref{sec:discussion}). With the correction, DANN, SDAT, MIC and CDAN+E end within three points of each other, while MCC stays below. Our method, CDAN+E with the correction, is the highest at \XpFullAcc\pct, but its lead is within the interval: \XdFullVsDannRead\ points above corrected DANN over five seeds (\XdFullVsDannReadLo\ to \XdFullVsDannReadHi), \XdFullVsSdatRead\ above SDAT and \XdFullVsMicRead\ above MIC. The choice of aligner matters less than the correction added to it. FDA~\cite{fda2020}, which gives the training images the stream's low frequencies, gains half as much as the correction alone (supplement).

\subsection{What each part contributes}
\label{sec:ablation}

\Cref{tab:ablation} adds the parts one at a time. The correction alone gains about twelve points without any training (b). Alignment alone gains more (c), and the two add up: the full method is better than either part alone (d). Subtracting the whole estimate from the full method's test images, fine pattern included, lowers its accuracy by about forty points, far below the recipe, because the fine band of an estimate built from a few objects still carries their imprints. Narrower low-passes ($\sigma$ of 8 or 16\,px) lose ten to twenty points, and wider ones (66 or 132\,px) lose up to two. The width was set on the fold that holds out D, whose 20 sessions are part of this sweep. With DANN in place of CDAN+E the method loses less than a point (e). The fold columns show where the gains are: on the two folds that cross units every part helps, and on the fold that holds out C, four other-gel sessions and one of wood, nothing does.

Two confidence objectives ask the classifier to be confident on the new hardware while spreading its predictions over the classes. Applied to the average prediction of several touches, as MEMO~\cite{zhang2022memo} does for augmented copies, the objective adds a few points with an interval that includes zero (f). Applied to every touch, as SHOT~\cite{liang2020shot} does, it costs about five (g). The method includes neither. Correcting the training images as well costs about two points (h).

A robot that adapts to its own stream classifies some touches that the stream already contained, so row d is transductive. To separate adaptation from memorization, we align with the first half of each configuration's test sessions only and score the second half, which the stream never contained (i). On those \XpLateN\ sessions the correction alone scores \XpLateReadAcc\pct. Alignment with the first half raises it to \XpLateEarlyAcc\pct, and alignment with a stream that contains the sessions to \XpLateFullAcc\pct, so about three quarters of the gain is adaptation to the hardware rather than to the test touches themselves.

\subsection{Other encoders and views}
\label{sec:encoders}

\Cref{tab:encoders} adds other encoders and a small CNN trained from scratch. On tube crops every encoder collapses under the recipe, the correction lifts each by ten to twenty points, and alignment adds to the correction for every encoder. The frame view uses 384-px crops placed anywhere in the frame, which contain much of the illumination field that tube crops only sample. There, alignment alone recovers most of what the correction recovers on tube crops, and what the correction adds depends on the encoder. It adds \XdDinoFrameReadVsCdan\ points for DINOv2-B (\XdDinoFrameReadVsCdanLo\ to \XdDinoFrameReadVsCdanHi), leaves ConvNeXt-T within a point, and costs ResNet-50 nine points (\XdResFrameReadVsCdanLo\ to \XdResFrameReadVsCdanHi). The best frame-view model, ConvNeXt-T, reaches \XpCnxFrameCdanReadAcc\pct, close to the ResNet-50 on tube crops, without help from the correction. The tube view is the pre-registered one and the frame view a later check.

\subsection{Sensors with broader appearance differences}
\label{sec:second}

SITR's Minis see different parts of their gels and differ in contrast by a factor of \SmContrastRatio\ (supplement). Subtracting the mean image of the test Mini, in the full band, lets a frozen DINOv2 with an MLP head recover from \CfSitrRawOneCross\ to \CfSitrStreamOneCross\pct\ on the classes learned on the other Mini when one class is tied to the test Mini (\cref{fig:confound}). It leaves a CNN trained from scratch at about \SmStreamCross\pct\ (supplement), because that CNN also learns the field of view and the contrast, which no per-pixel correction removes. We tie four classes to the test Mini and train a CNN from scratch, with its unlabeled images as the stream (supplement). CDAN+E then raises the accuracy on the 16 classes learned on the other Mini from \XsJit\ to \XsCdan\pct. With only 5 of the 20 classes in the stream the gain shrinks by about half, so the stream must cover the robot's materials.

\subsection{Dating the changes}
\label{sec:trackerexp}

Run over all \EpRecorded\ episodes in recording order, the detector raises \FrAlarms\ alarms: \FrUnitLatency\ episodes after the change of unit, \FrGelLatency\ after the gel replacement, and \FrFalseAlarms\ false ones. On streams spliced from our recordings and from three public datasets it detects \TeOursFramesDet\pct\ of the changes in ours and \TeSitrMiniDet\ to \TeTmnistDet\pct\ in the others (supplement). Restarting the estimate at its alarms instead of the true dates changes the full method's accuracy by \XdFullVarDetVsLp\ points (\XdFullVarDetVsLpLo\ to \XdFullVarDetVsLpHi).
