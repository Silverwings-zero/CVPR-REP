\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\paragraph{Protocol.}
Material: train on campaigns A+B+C (June--July, 1{,}360 episodes), test on D (September--October, 1{,}351 episodes, 20 materials, chance 5\pct); inputs are single native 192-px crops (11.3\,\si{\micro\metre}/px; 12 per episode; frame integration and resolution are ablated in \cref{sec:ablation}); per episode, logits of its crops are pooled; we report macro accuracy, macro-F1 (collapse onto a few classes leaves macro accuracy unchanged and F1 not) and the share of test episodes assigned to the most predicted class. Heads are MLPs on frozen embeddings, three seeds; labels are used only in the heads. Because campaign D records every material in exactly one session, materials are the unit of statistics: we give paired bootstrap intervals over the 20 materials for the claims we make. The era probe (given the material, which campaign) measures day-identifiability of a representation on the training campaigns (chance \num{57.7}\pct, lower is better).

\subsection{Material across months}
\label{sec:material}

\Cref{fig:material} and \cref{tab:material} give the main result. Raw, the supervised CNN scores \af{\SupFoneRawSAcc}{\SupFoneRawSF}\ (accuracy\,/\,macro-F1, three seeds; \SupFoneRawSTop\pct\ of episodes in one class), frozen Sparsh-DINO, -MAE and -I-JEPA \SdinoRawAcc, \MaeRawAcc\ and \IjepaRawAcc, a TactSpace replica \TactSpaceRawAcc, and native-resolution normal-map alignment---the calibration-free physics target of \cite{unipsgel2026}---\NmRawAcc, all with the same collapse. Stream calibration with the previous session's state lifts the supervised CNN to \af{\SupFonePrevSAcc}{\SupFonePrevSF}\ ($\pm$\SupFonePrevSAccSd\ over seeds) and Sparsh-I-JEPA to \af{\IjepaPrevAcc}{\IjepaPrevF}\ (paired bootstrap over materials $+$\num{24.2}, 95\pct\ CI $[+8.2, +41.5]$); the gain is largest when the previous session was recorded the same day (supervised, eight-frame inputs: \num{14.9}$\rightarrow$\num{40.4} over 11 such sessions, vs \num{30.1} for other-day sources), as the measurement predicts. Starting from the previous state and updating it online helps from the first contact; estimating the state from the current session alone needs 10--20 contacts.

\paragraph{Photometric jitter.} Standard photometric augmentation (per-channel gain $\pm$20\pct\ and a smooth multiplicative field $\pm$15\pct) is, with single-frame inputs, a strong baseline on its own: \af{\SupAugFoneRawSAcc}{\SupAugFoneRawSF}\ raw (three seeds, $\pm$\SupAugFoneRawSAccSd) and \af{\SupAugFonePrevSAcc}{\SupAugFonePrevSF}\ with the previous session's state---the best deployable pipeline when labels are available. The same augmentation on eight-frame inputs gives only \SupAugRawSAcc\ raw (\cref{sec:ablation}): the jitter removes the low-frequency photometric part of the day signature, and integration re-introduces its fine-scale part.

\paragraph{Without labels.} The label-free state-swap adapter needs neither labels nor test-time data: it lifts frozen Sparsh-I-JEPA to \af{\IjepaAdAcc}{\IjepaAdF}\ (three seeds, $\pm$\IjepaAdAccSd; $+$\num{24.0} $[+13.9, +35.9]$), Sparsh-MAE to \MaeAdAcc\ and Sparsh-DINO to \SdinoAdAcc, with the era probe falling from \num{77}--\num{79} to \num{66}--\num{69}. The same objective from scratch gives \af{\SwapFoneRawSAcc}{\SwapFoneRawSF}\ (three seeds). On the general-vision DINOv2~\cite{oquab2024dinov2} the adapter adds only \num{3.8} $[-4.3,+11.5]$: the objective reorganises what tactile pretraining already holds. It is the best option without labels; with labels, jitter and calibration do better.

\paragraph{Adaptation baselines.}
AdaBN~\cite{li2018adabn} re-estimated over the whole test campaign reaches \SupAdaAcc\ for the eight-frame supervised CNN---but that pools 20 objects into one state. Re-estimated per session, which is what a robot has, it gives \SupAdaSessAcc\pct\ for the supervised CNN and 4--9\pct\ for every other model: with one object per session the statistics absorb the object.

\subsection{Normal force: geometry survives, appearance does not}
\label{sec:force}

\begin{figure}[t]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig/fig_force.pdf}
  \caption{\textbf{Force across days needs geometry against the unloaded gel.} $R^2$ of log normal force on later days.}
  \label{fig:force}
\end{figure}

Normal force cannot be read from appearance across days: whole-frame representations trained on native frames---label-free, supervised, and with the force signal in the objective---reach $R^2\le\ForceLearnedBestR$ on the later campaign, and model-free deformation statistics against a fresh bare-gel frame reach \num{0.22}. Marker spreading against the bare-gel marker grid does transfer (\cref{fig:force}): within a session the affine divergence tracks the Nano17 force at Spearman \ForceWithinRho; a linear map of three affine statistics fitted on the first 13 sessions of campaign D predicts the last 7 at $R^2$ \ForceGeomFreshR\ ($r$ \ForceGeomFreshC) with a fresh grid and \ForceGeomPrevR\ ($r$ \ForceGeomPrevC) with the previous session's grid; fitted on June--July with a single bare-gel grid borrowed from September, it predicts September--October at $R^2$ \ForceGeomXeraR\ ($r$ \ForceGeomXeraC). A new gel keeps the force ranking ($r\approx0.70$) but changes the force-to-displacement scale: a gel replacement changes mechanics as well as rendering, and absolute force needs one scale calibration per gel.

\subsection{Contact}
\label{sec:contact}
Contact detectors trained on frozen whole-frame features on the seven lead-in sessions detect contact in the other sessions at $\ge$98\pct\ recall but reject \ContactRawSpec\pct\ of those sessions' bare-gel frames: another day's unloaded gel looks like contact. Referencing the embedding to the session's own bare gel restores \ContactRefSpec\pct\ specificity at \ContactRefRecall\pct\ recall (differencing the raw frame against the blank: \ContactBlankdiffSpec\pct). The unloaded state is the reference for contact as for force.

\subsection{Second unit}
\label{sec:second}
On Real Tactile MNIST (digit from single touches, 10-way, two events: later days on the same gel; the gel swap), every encoder again collapses raw (supervised \TmLaterSupRawTop\pct\ of predictions in one class, frozen Sparsh \TmLaterIjepaRawTop\pct) and recovers with the previous run's state (supervised F1 \TmLaterSupRawF$\rightarrow$\TmLaterSupPrevF; frozen Sparsh \TmLaterIjepaRawAcc$\rightarrow$\TmLaterIjepaPrevAcc\pct; across the gel swap \TmGelIjepaRawAcc$\rightarrow$\TmGelIjepaPrevAcc). Per-run AdaBN works there (\TmLaterSupAdaRunAcc\pct) because every run holds all ten digits---the control for the per-session collapse on our data. The label-free swap did not transfer to coarse digit shape on this dataset; we claim it for texture on our unit.

\subsection{Ablations}
\label{sec:ablation}
\paragraph{Frames integrated.} The imprint is static during a stroke, so consecutive native frames can be averaged; this raises the reliability of fine texture but lowers cross-day accuracy. With the supervised CNN and previous-session calibration: one frame \SupFonePrevSAcc\pct\ $\rightarrow$ two \SupFtwoPrevAcc\ $\rightarrow$ four \SupFfourPrevAcc\ $\rightarrow$ eight \SupPrevAcc; with jitter, raw \SupAugFoneRawSAcc\ (one) vs \SupAugRawSAcc\ (eight). Integration makes the stable fine-scale content---the day signature---easier to learn; raw accuracy without jitter is flat. The label-free objective is insensitive to it (\SwapFoneRawSAcc\ vs \SwapRawAcc).
\paragraph{Resolution.} Area-downscaling the native crops twofold does not reduce cross-day material accuracy (single frame, calibrated: \SupFoneScHalfPrevSAcc\ vs \SupFonePrevSAcc\ native; eight frames: \SupScHalfOwnAcc\ vs \SupOwnAcc); fourfold costs \num{1.5}--\num{3} points (\SupFoneScQuarterPrevSAcc; \SupScQuarterOwnAcc). The material cue that survives days lives above $\sim$45\,\si{\micro\metre}; the finest band is unreliable in one frame (0.14) and mostly signature when integrated.
\paragraph{Decoys.} Removing the position-matched decoy negatives from the adapter objective costs \num{3.1} points $[+0.5,+6.1]$ (\IjepaAdNoDecoyAcc\ vs \IjepaAdAcc).
