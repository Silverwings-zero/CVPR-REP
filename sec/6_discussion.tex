\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the results show.}
When classes are tied to hardware, as one object per session ties them, a classifier uses the hardware state as a class feature, and an evaluation within one collection cannot see it. Testing on sessions recorded on other hardware and reporting the share of test samples in the most predicted class make the failure visible, and a robot can repair much of it with its own unlabelled contacts.

\paragraph{Where the reading costs accuracy.}
The method repairs the shortcut, not every effect of new hardware. The reading lowers the accuracy on the eight sessions recorded on the other gel of the same unit, both alone and on top of alignment. These sessions hold four rigid materials, recorded in July on gel 2 and in October on gel 3, each among the first sessions on its gel, where the estimate holds few other objects, so what follows is tentative. A better estimate does not help. Keeping the unit's estimate across the gel replacement, which would be right if the smooth field belonged mostly to the unit, lowers this stratum further. On these sessions the shortcut points to the right answer, because the test images carry the illumination of the unit on which their materials were learned, and any reading removes part of that cue.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
We followed two units and three gels of one sensor model. The public datasets add seven further GelSight Minis and hand-held DIGIT data, and only Tactile MNIST and HCT~\cite{tvl2024} span more than a few days. HCT ties materials to sessions too, but its shift between days is mostly a change of objects rather than of the sensor's look, and our method does not help there (supplement). Alignment needs a stream that covers the materials the robot is expected to recognise.
No material was recorded on the same unit and gel months apart, so stability over months is shown for the state itself and not for a classifier. Lubricant is re-applied in step with use, so slow drift with use cannot be separated from lubricant residue. The estimate counts every session once and so needs to know when the robot moves to a new object, which a robot knows from its task.
