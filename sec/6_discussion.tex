\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the results show.}
A VBTS image contains the hardware state of its unit and gel. In our recordings the state stayed stable over months of use and was replaced when the unit or the gel was replaced, and the hardware is just as identifiable in the images of two public datasets. A classifier trained with one object per session can use the state as a class feature, and after a change of hardware it collapses onto the classes recorded under the new state. Frozen tactile foundation models encode the unit almost perfectly and collapse in the same way. A robot can estimate the state from its own contacts with different objects, detect changes of hardware with thresholds fixed in advance, and read each image relative to the estimate. On our data this undoes the collapse for supervised and frozen encoders, and test-time adaptation methods that assume mixed batches do not.

\paragraph{What they do not show.}
The correction is not a general remedy for a change of hardware. Within states seen in training it is unnecessary and can cost up to 10 points. For a training set in which no class is tied to one hardware state, it adds less, and nothing to a CNN trained with photometric jitter. It did not repair the materials that cross our gel replacement in either direction except wood in fold D, and we cannot tell whether the gel, the materials or the structure of the training set is responsible. The state also says nothing about mechanics. Normal force transferred only when read from marker geometry against the unloaded gel, and on FEATS no image-space reading carried force across units or gels. The label-free adapter uses no material labels, but it uses session identity, which in our data identifies the object, and it did not transfer to digit shape on Tactile MNIST.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
We followed two units and three gels of one sensor model. The public datasets add further units and gels for detection, but only Tactile MNIST spans more than a few days. No material was recorded on the same unit and gel months apart, so stability over months is shown for the state itself and not for a classifier. All design choices were made on campaign D, and the other folds were run afterwards. Lubricant is re-applied in step with use, so slow drift with use cannot be separated from lubricant residue. The estimator needs to know which contacts belong to the same object, which our sessions provide. Absolute accuracies are modest for a 20-way problem on single crops with one session per material.

\paragraph{Outlook.}
Every VBTS dataset recorded in sittings carries its hardware history in its images, and evaluations within one collection cannot see it. Holding out sessions recorded on other hardware, reporting how many test samples fall into the most predicted class, and estimating the state from the deployment's own contacts are simple practices that would make this failure visible and, where it comes from a shortcut, repairable.
