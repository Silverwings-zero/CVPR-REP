\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the results show.}
A VBTS image contains the hardware state of its unit and gel. In our recordings the state stayed stable over months of use and was replaced when the unit or the gel was replaced, and the hardware is just as identifiable in the images of public datasets. When classes are tied to hardware, as one object per session ties them, a classifier uses the state as a class feature. We showed this both on our recordings and by injecting the tie into SITR, where a single tied class halves the accuracy on all others. Frozen tactile foundation models encode the unit almost perfectly and collapse in the same way. Reading each image relative to a running estimate of the state breaks the tie at test time. It undoes most of the collapse and costs at most a point or two where the hardware was seen in training. Re-rendering training images under other sessions' states breaks the tie at the source, but it helps less and adds nothing on top of the reading.

\paragraph{What they do not show.}
The tools repair the shortcut, not every effect of new hardware. A CNN trained on June alone loses about 30 points on the other unit, and no state estimate recovers them. A CNN trained from scratch on either confounded public set learns the sensor from cues that the additive corrections do not remove. Across our gel replacement, the change was additive enough for a bare-gel correction to recover most of the four rigid materials, but the tracker could not, because the campaign that tests them begins with no other objects on the new hardware. The state also says nothing about mechanics, and normal force transferred only when read from marker geometry against the unloaded gel.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
We followed two units and three gels of one sensor model, and all public datasets we used are GelSight Mini data. Only Tactile MNIST spans more than a few days. No material was recorded on the same unit and gel months apart, so stability over months is shown for the state itself and not for a classifier. All design choices were made on campaign D. Two of them followed failures there: the detector moved from the material crops to whole frames, and the training reference moved from the previous session to the mean of the same hardware. A reversed test, written down before its models were trained, confirmed all three of its predictions. Lubricant is re-applied in step with use, so slow drift with use cannot be separated from lubricant residue. The estimator weights objects equally, which needs to know which contacts belong to the same object. Our sessions provide this, and weighting contacts instead costs the CNN about three points. Absolute accuracies are modest for a 20-way problem on single crops with one session per material.

\paragraph{Outlook.}
Every GelSight Mini dataset we examined carries its hardware history in its images, and evaluations within one collection cannot see it. Holding out sessions recorded on other hardware, reporting how many test samples fall into the most predicted class, and breaking the tie between class and state, during training or at test time, are simple practices that would make this failure visible and repairable.
