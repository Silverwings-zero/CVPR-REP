\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the representation is.}
A tactile representation for robot learning has to carry the sensor's state, because the state changes with the rig, the power cycles and the gel, and dominates the fine-scale content of every image. Ours factors each observation into state and touch and keeps two states the robot can maintain from its own stream---the loaded state from recent grasps, the unloaded state from the bare gel between them---and reads each cue against the right one: appearance against the loaded state, geometry and contact against the unloaded one. This is general in the ways that matter downstream: it applies on top of any encoder, including frozen foundation models, it serves the three quantities a grasping policy needs, it can be learned without labels, it is safe---it costs nothing when the state has not changed, where test-time normalisation loses 7--16 points---and it transferred to a second sensor unit. It is deliberately not a single network that absorbs all three cues: every attempt to learn force from appearance, including with the force signal in the objective, failed across days, and factoring the cues is the stronger design.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Our own recordings come from one sensor unit; the signature and the calibration transfer to a second unit in public data, the label-free swap does not transfer to coarse shape there. Each session holds one material, so material identity can only be tested across campaigns, and the five materials recorded on the new gel are also those with a single training session, so the gel effect on material recognition cannot be isolated; it is shown through the state fields, the force scale and contact instead. Absolute accuracies are modest for a 20-way zero-shot problem, and the stateful pipeline recovers most but not all of the within-campaign level; a few target-campaign labels stack with it. Every design choice was selected on campaign D; the within-campaign hold-out confirms the directions that matter but is not a second cross-campaign test. The label-free objective is weak from scratch; its value is on a pretrained encoder, and we have shown it for texture only. Lubricant is re-applied in step with use, so slow drift with use cannot be separated from lubricant residue.

\paragraph{Outlook.}
The sensor's state is present in every VBTS dataset recorded in sittings, which is all of them, and it is invisible to within-collection evaluation. Representing it explicitly, holding out a later campaign of the same unit, and estimating the state from the deployment's own contacts where no blank frames exist are cheap changes that we hope become standard.
