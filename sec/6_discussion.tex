\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{The gel-only sessions.}
Correction reduces accuracy on the eight gel-only transfer sessions, both alone and with alignment. These cover four rigid materials recorded early on each gel, confounding material, gel, and estimate maturity; they do not establish the cause. Gel replacement also changes appearance beyond the smooth component: re-rendering July sessions using the difference between the gels' bare-gel frames raises CNN accuracy from \GelRrCSupRaw\ to \GelRrCSupRr\pct\ (\supp{M}). This suggests that a bare-gel reference captures changes the contact-based correction misses.

\paragraph{Practical limits.}
The estimate needs contacts with several different objects and known session boundaries. Alignment needs a stream that covers the robot's materials (\cref{sec:second}). No material was recorded on the same unit and gel months apart, so recognition over months is untested, and wear cannot be separated from lubricant residue. The failure we study needs the new hardware to be tied to a few classes in the training set, as it is in every fold. We followed two units and three gels of one sensor model, with one change of unit and one of gel, and on HCT~\cite{tvl2024}, where days differ mainly in objects, the method lowers accuracy (\supp{T}).
