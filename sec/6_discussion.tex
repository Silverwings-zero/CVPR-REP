\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What this means for evaluation.}
A tactile classifier can be accurate for the wrong reason. When each class is recorded on one piece of hardware, the signature of that hardware identifies the class, and a train/test split of the same recordings carries the signature on both sides. Such a split rewards a model for using the signature and cannot reveal it. To expose the dependence before the hardware changes, a dataset should record which unit and gel produced every image, and an evaluation should hold out hardware and report how predictions spread over classes. A system that learns from touch should treat its sensor's state as part of its input.

\paragraph{The gel-only sessions.}
On the eight sessions that cross only a gel, the correction lowers the accuracy, alone and on top of alignment. We observe this drop, but eight sessions do not establish its cause. They hold four rigid materials, each among the first sessions on its gel, so material, gel and the age of the estimate change together. Perhaps the shortcut is right here: these materials were learned on the unit that tests them, so the illumination that misleads the classifier elsewhere points to the correct classes. Or a new gel changes the image beyond its smooth component. We therefore claim no benefit after a gel change alone.

\paragraph{Practical limits.}
The estimate separates the hardware from the touch only by averaging over different objects, so a robot that handles one object, or a few alike, would subtract part of their imprint, and it must know when its object changes. Alignment needs a stream that covers the robot's materials (\cref{sec:second}). A stable signature is also not a stable classifier. No material was recorded on the same unit and gel months apart, so recognition over months is untested, and wear cannot be separated from lubricant residue. We followed two units and three gels of one sensor model, and on HCT~\cite{tvl2024}, where days differ mainly in objects, the method does not help (supplement).
