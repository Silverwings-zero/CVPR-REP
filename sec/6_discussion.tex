\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the results show.}
A VBTS image contains the hardware state of its unit and gel. In our recordings the state was stable over months of use and was replaced with the unit or the gel. When classes are tied to hardware, as one object per session ties them, a classifier uses the state as a class feature. This holds on our recordings, in a reversed split whose outcome we predicted in advance, and in SITR when we inject the tie. Frozen tactile foundation models encode the unit and collapse in the same way. Reading each image relative to a running estimate of the state breaks the tie at test time. It undoes most of the collapse, does as well as a bare-gel frame, and costs at most a point or two where the hardware was seen in training. What matters is that the estimate averages many recent objects. A mean that forgets old objects does as well without the detector, which then serves to date the changes. Re-rendering the training images under other states helps less on our data and adds nothing on top of the reading, although on SITR with a frozen encoder it does slightly better than the reading.

\paragraph{What they do not show.}
The tools repair the shortcut, not every effect of new hardware. Against AdaBN, the reading is clearly better on our main split but not significantly better on the reversed one. A CNN trained on June alone, with one session per material, loses about 30 points on the other unit, and no state estimate recovers them. The reading removes the additive part of the state. SITR's Minis also differ in field of view and contrast, and a CNN trained from scratch still recognises them after the reading. After our gel replacement, a bare-gel correction recovered most of the four rigid materials, but the tracker could not, because the campaign that tests them begins with no other objects on the new hardware. On HCT, holding out sessions halves the accuracy of a frozen encoder, and neither HCT's no-contact frames nor our estimate recover it, so a session tie can hurt through more than the additive state.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
We followed two units and three gels of one sensor model. The public datasets add seven further GelSight Minis and hand-held DIGIT data, and only Tactile MNIST and HCT span more than a few days. No material was recorded on the same unit and gel months apart, so stability over months is shown for the state itself and not for a classifier. Our design choices were made on campaign D, two of them after failures there, and the reversed split is our only test fixed in advance. Lubricant is re-applied in step with use, so slow drift with use cannot be separated from lubricant residue. The estimator weights objects equally and so needs to know which contacts belong to one object, although weighting contacts instead costs the CNN only about three points. Absolute accuracies are modest for a 20-way problem on single crops with one session per material.

\paragraph{Outlook.}
Every GelSight Mini dataset we examined carries its hardware history in its images, and evaluations within one collection cannot see it. Holding out sessions recorded on other hardware, reporting the share of test samples in the most predicted class, and breaking the tie between class and state are simple practices that make this failure visible and, where the state is additive, repairable.
